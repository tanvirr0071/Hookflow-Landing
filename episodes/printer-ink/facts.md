# Printer Ink — Facts (Markup: how a business really makes its money)

Researched 2026-10-23. Newest full-year figures: HP fiscal 2025 (year ended October 31, 2025; 10-K filed with SEC). HP's fiscal 2026 ends October 31, 2026 and full-year results are not out yet, so the newest data after FY2025 is fiscal Q3 2026 (quarter ended July 31, 2026).
Labels: VERIFIED = primary source or two reliable sources. REPORTED = one reliable source. CAREFUL = true only in the exact wording given. MYTH = popular but wrong.

## Facts

1. **Which? (UK, September 2022): an Epson ink set worked out at £1.78 per ml, over seven times Dom Pérignon at 24p per ml (£180 a bottle).** VERIFIED (primary, Which? press release, 23 September 2022).
   The cartridge was for the Epson Workforce Pro WF3820DWF: a replacement set cost £111.99 from Epson.co.uk, over £330 a year at three refills. Third-party sets cost under £140 for three, a saving of over £180. Prices correct as of 6 September 2022.
   CAREFUL: these are UK prices, an Epson cartridge (not HP), and "up to" seven times is the single worst case Which? found. Safe wording: "In Britain, the consumer group Which? found one Epson ink set cost more than seven times as much per millilitre as Dom Pérignon champagne."
   URL: https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/which-calls-for-action-over-printer-inks-costing-up-to-seven-times-more-than-dom-perignon-champagne-aeHrW6g7B3vb

2. **Which? survey: 49% of printer owners keep buying branded ink; only 4% of inkjet owners said a third-party cartridge would not work.** REPORTED (primary survey, one source).
   Online survey of 9,522 Which? Connect members, 23 March to 4 April 2022 (7,524 asked about ink). An HP spokesperson told Which?: "Dynamic security is a process that authenticates print supplies to prevent the use of cartridges without an original HP chip." Which? asked the UK competition regulator (CMA) to investigate.
   Safe wording: "In a UK survey, about half of printer owners said they still buy the brand's own ink."
   URL: https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/which-calls-for-action-over-printer-inks-costing-up-to-seven-times-more-than-dom-perignon-champagne-aeHrW6g7B3vb

3. **US prices: Consumer Reports found cartridge ink from about $13 an ounce to about $95 an ounce. HP's 64XL black held 0.45 ounce and cost $42, or $93 an ounce.** REPORTED (Consumer Reports' own testing, article dated August 17, 2018).
   CR: even the cheapest cartridge ink "costs more than twice as much as Dom Pérignon Champagne." The same maker's 952XL came to $15.38 an ounce, so price per ounce varies about six-fold by cartridge.
   CAREFUL: 2018 prices. Say "in 2018, Consumer Reports found..." Do not present $93 as today's price.
   URL: https://www.consumerreports.org/printers/the-high-cost-of-wasted-printer-ink

4. **Much of the ink you buy never reaches the paper.** REPORTED (Consumer Reports testing, 2018).
   CR: in intermittent use, "many models delivered half or less of their ink to the page," and "a few managed no more than 20 to 30 percent." The rest goes to cleaning and maintenance cycles.
   Safe wording: "Consumer Reports found that if you print now and then, many inkjets put half or less of their ink on the page."
   URL: https://www.consumerreports.org/printers/the-high-cost-of-wasted-printer-ink

5. **HP fiscal 2025: Printing revenue $16.70 billion; Supplies (ink and toner) $10.92 billion, about 65% of it.** VERIFIED (primary, HP 10-K for FY ended October 31, 2025).
   Printing net revenue $16,702M (FY2024 $17,338M; FY2023 $18,029M), down 3.7%. Supplies $10,916M (FY2024 $11,295M). Commercial Printing hardware $4,633M; Consumer Printing hardware $1,153M. HP total net revenue $55,295M, so printing is about 30% of HP.
   Math (my arithmetic, not printed by HP): $10.916B / 365 = about $30 million a day in supplies; $10,916M / $16,702M = 65.4%.
   CAREFUL: "Supplies" means ink AND toner (and other consumables), not ink alone. Safe wording: "Last year HP sold about $10.9 billion of ink and toner. That's roughly $30 million a day, and almost two-thirds of its whole printing business." Home printer hardware was only $1.15 billion.
   URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47217/000004721725000071/hpq-20251031.htm

6. **Printing earns HP more profit than PCs on less than half the sales (FY2025).** VERIFIED (primary, HP 10-K).
   Printing: $16.7B revenue, $3,118M earnings from operations, 18.7% margin. Personal Systems (PCs): $38,532M revenue, $2,054M earnings from operations, 5.3% margin. Printing is about 60% of the two segments' combined operating earnings ($3.118B of $5.172B, my arithmetic).
   Safe wording: "HP's PC sales are more than twice its printing sales, but printing still makes it about a billion dollars more in operating profit."
   URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47217/000004721725000071/hpq-20251031.htm

7. **Latest quarter (fiscal Q3 2026, released August 26, 2026): Printing $3.9B, down 2%; Supplies down 3%; consumer printer units down 9%; margin 18.1%.** VERIFIED (primary, HP earnings release filed with SEC).
   The release quotes "Bruce Broussard, Interim CEO." Total HP revenue that quarter: $15.7B.
   Safe wording: "Even as fewer people buy home printers, HP's printing business still keeps about 18 cents of every dollar as operating profit."
   URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47217/000004721726000048/hp73126exhibit991q326.htm

   Quotes as reported: "Every time a customer buys a printer, it's an investment for us." ... "if this customer doesn't print enough or doesn't use our supplies, it's a bad investment." (PCWorld wording; The Register prints "that customer".) Yahoo Tech: HP's goal is "to reduce the number of what we call unprofitable customers." PCWorld says the interview was CNBC's Squawk Box during the World Economic Forum in Davos.
   CAREFUL: no exact air date confirmed. Say "in January 2024." Lores left HP in February 2026, so say "HP's then-CEO."

    This is how HP's own CEO described the razor-and-blades model. Use it only as a quote attributed to Lores in 2024, not as a fact about every printer (see fact 12 and myth 4).

    Big tank printers "typically have a higher initial margin" than cartridge printers but "do not result in follow-on sales of high margin traditional ink cartridges." HP also lists "increased digitization, hybrid work" as reasons printing demand is falling.
    Safe wording: "In its own annual report, HP calls its ink cartridges 'high margin.'"
    URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47217/000004721725000071/hpq-20251031.htm

13. **The 2016 "Dynamic Security" update: printers rejected non-HP cartridges, HP paid $1.5 million in the US.** VERIFIED (The Register and Action Intell, plus court caption).
    Customers saw messages saying cartridges were "damaged or missing"; The Register says HP later admitted those messages were wrong and the cut-off date in the case was 13 September 2016. Action Intell: "HP agrees under the settlement not to reactivate Dynamic Security in the Class Printers and to pay $1.5 million." HP denied wrongdoing. Australia: HP's local arm compensated affected buyers A$50 each (The Register, 2018).
    URLs: https://www.theregister.com/2018/09/21/hp_should_pay_out_15m_for_printer_bricking_update/ and https://www.action-intell.com/2019/01/08/notice-provided-to-settlement-class-in-in-hp-printer-firmware-update-litigation/

## Common myths

1. **MYTH: "HP ink costs seven times more than Dom Pérignon."**
   True: the seven-times figure is from Which? in the UK (2022), in pounds, for an Epson ink set, and it is the worst case (fact 1). For a US version, use Consumer Reports (2018): even the cheapest US cartridge ink was more than twice Dom Pérignon per ounce, and HP's 64XL worked out to $93 an ounce (fact 3).

2. **MYTH: "Using third-party ink voids your printer warranty."**
   True: in the US, FTC staff said in April 2018 that telling consumers they "must use specified parts or service providers to keep their warranties intact" is generally prohibited by the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act. A maker can still refuse to cover damage actually caused by the third-party part. VERIFIED (primary, FTC). URL: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2018/04/ftc-staff-warns-companies-it-illegal-condition-warranty-coverage-use-specified-parts-or-services

3. **MYTH: "HP printers block all non-HP ink."**
   True: HP says Dynamic Security blocks "cartridges without an original HP chip" (fact 2). Under the 2025 US settlement HP must keep letting users decline firmware updates that include it (fact 14). Which?'s survey found only 4% of inkjet owners had a third-party cartridge fail.

4. **MYTH: "Every printer is sold at a loss."**
   True: HP's own executives say it loses money on about 25% of customers, not all of them (fact 11), and its 10-K says ink-tank printers carry a "higher initial margin" (fact 12). The loss is on customers who don't buy HP supplies.

5. **MYTH: "The printer business is dying, so ink doesn't matter to HP anymore."**
   True: unit sales are falling (consumer printer units down 9% in fiscal Q3 2026, fact 7), but printing still made $3.1 billion in operating profit in FY2025, about $1 billion more than HP's PC business (fact 6).

## Strongest hook number
**About $30 million a day.** HP sold $10.9 billion of ink and toner in fiscal 2025, roughly $30 million every day, and nearly two-thirds of its printing revenue (fact 5). Say "ink and toner."
Alternate (US, dated): in 2018 Consumer Reports found HP 64XL ink cost $93 an ounce, and even the cheapest cartridge ink cost more than twice Dom Pérignon (fact 3).

## Strongest twist
HP is best known for laptops, but its printing arm, with less than half the revenue, made about a billion dollars more operating profit than its PCs in FY2025 (fact 6). And HP's own CEO said the customers who refuse to buy its ink are "a bad investment" (fact 8): the printer is the bait, the cartridge is the business.
