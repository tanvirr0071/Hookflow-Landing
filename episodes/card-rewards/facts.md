# Facts: Credit-card rewards (who really pays for them)

Series: Markup. Research date: 2026-10-23.

Labels: VERIFIED = primary source or two reliable sources. REPORTED = one reliable source. CAREFUL = true only in the wording given. MYTH = popular but wrong.

---

## Facts

### 1. 2025 swipe fees: about $198 billion (newest full year)
- **Label:** REPORTED. The figure comes from Nilson Report data released by a merchant lobby, the Merchants Payments Coalition (MPC), in March 2026. Several trade outlets carried it, but all from the same release. I could not open the Nilson Report itself.
- **Detail:** Credit and debit swipe fees in 2025 were $198.25B, up 5.9% from $187.2B in 2024. Credit cards (all brands) were $157.8B and debit cards were $40.5B.
- **Safe wording:** "US merchants paid about $198 billion in card swipe fees in 2025, according to Nilson Report figures cited by merchant groups."
- **Quote:** Doug Kantor, MPC executive committee member and general counsel of the National Association of Convenience Stores, said in the March 2026 release: "Credit card swipe fees make just about everything Americans buy more expensive."
- **URLs:**
  - https://pdrmag.com/record-credit-card-swipe-fees-fuel-calls-for-credit-card-competition-act-reform/ (names Nilson as the source)
  - https://chaindrugreview.com/swipe-fees-hit-record-198b-as-reform-efforts-grow/ (March 18, 2026)
  - https://nrf.com/advocacy/policy-issues/swipe-fees

### 2. The 2024 total depends on who counts: $236.4B or $187.2B
- **Label:** CAREFUL. The two figures measure different things.
- **Detail:**
  - CMSPI, a consultancy that works for merchants, put 2024 swipe fees at $236.4B (report released September 2025). It counts interchange, network fees and processing fees.
  - Nilson put 2024 at $187.2B and does not specify which fee parts it counts.
  - No CMSPI estimate for 2025 was found.
- **Safe wording:** "Estimates for 2024 range from $187 billion (Nilson Report) to $236 billion (CMSPI, a consultancy that works for merchants)." Never present $236.4B as the official or only number.
- **URL:** https://nsga.org/news/swipe-fees-totaled-236-billion-in-2024/

### 3. Cost per family: "about $1,200" or "nearly $1,800" a year
- **Label:** CAREFUL. Both are estimates from merchant-side groups.
- **Detail:**
  - CMSPI: nearly $1,800 per average family in 2024.
  - Based on Nilson data: nearly $1,200.
  - The National Retail Federation (NRF), which lobbies for swipe-fee reform, uses "more than $1,200 a year" per household.
- **Safe wording:** "Merchant groups estimate swipe fees add roughly $1,200 a year to the average household's costs."
- **URLs:**
  - https://nsga.org/news/swipe-fees-totaled-236-billion-in-2024/
  - https://nrf.com/advocacy/policy-issues/swipe-fees

### 4. Visa and Mastercard credit cards alone: $118.8B in 2025 at an average 2.36%
- **Label:** REPORTED. These are Nilson figures carried in the MPC release.
- **Detail:** Visa- and Mastercard-branded credit card fees were $118.8B in 2025, up from $111.2B in 2024. The average rate was 2.36% of the purchase, up from 2.35% in 2024 and 2.02% in 2010. NRF says Visa and Mastercard control over 80% of the market.
- **Safe wording:** "On a typical Visa or Mastercard credit purchase, the store gives up about 2.4% of the sale."
- **URLs:**
  - https://pdrmag.com/record-credit-card-swipe-fees-fuel-calls-for-credit-card-competition-act-reform/
  - https://nrf.com/advocacy/policy-issues/swipe-fees

### 5. Swipe fees have roughly tripled since 2009
- **Label:** REPORTED. The figures come from the merchant coalition's tracking.
- **Detail:** MPC began tracking at $62.1B in 2009. Visa/Mastercard credit fees rose from $25.6B in 2009 to $118.8B in 2025, which is +365%. NRF says fees have "roughly tripled since 2009."
- **Safe wording:** "Swipe fees have roughly tripled since 2009, according to merchant groups."
- **URLs:**
  - https://www.retailcustomerexperience.com/news/swipe-fees-hit-record-setting-19825b/ (March 31, 2026)
  - https://pdrmag.com/record-credit-card-swipe-fees-fuel-calls-for-credit-card-competition-act-reform/

### 6. Boston Fed: each cash-paying household pays $149 a year to card users
- **Label:** VERIFIED. This is a primary Federal Reserve Bank of Boston paper: Schuh, Shy and Stavins, "Who Gains and Who Loses from Credit Card Payments? Theory and Calibrations," Public Policy Discussion Paper No. 10-03, version of August 31, 2010.
- **Exact quote from the abstract:** "On average, each cash-using household pays $149 to card-using households and each card-using household receives $1,133 from cash users every year."
- **Caveat:** In this paper, "cash" means every payment that is not a credit card: cash, checks, debit and prepaid cards.
- **Safe wording:** "A Boston Fed study found the average household that doesn't pay by credit card hands about $149 a year to credit-card users, and each card-using household gets about $1,133 from them."
- **URLs:**
  - https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/public-policy-discussion-paper/2010/who-gains-and-who-loses-from-credit-card-payments-theory-and-calibrations.aspx
  - PDF: https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/Workingpapers/PDF/ppdp1003.pdf

### 7. Boston Fed: the poor pay, the rich collect
- **Label:** VERIFIED. Same paper, abstract.
- **Detail:** After counting the rewards banks pay out:
  - The lowest-income household ($20,000 or less a year) pays $21 a year.
  - The highest-income household ($150,000 or more) receives $750 a year.
- **Exact quote from the abstract:** "Reducing merchant fees and card rewards would likely increase consumer welfare."
- **Safe wording:** "In the Fed economists' estimate, the lowest-income households pay about $21 a year into the system, while the richest collect about $750."
- **URL:** https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/Workingpapers/PDF/ppdp1003.pdf

### 8. Boston Fed: how the cash payer ends up paying
- **Label:** VERIFIED. Same paper, abstract and introduction.
- **Exact quote:** The transfer happens "because merchants generally do not set differential prices for card users to recoup the costs of fees and rewards."
- **Detail:**
  - The introduction says merchants "mark up their retail prices for all consumers" to cover card fees.
  - It also says "about 79 percent of banks' revenue from credit card merchant fees is obtained from cash payers."
- **Safe wording:** "Stores raise prices for everyone to cover card fees, so people paying cash help fund the points."
- **URL:** https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/Workingpapers/PDF/ppdp1003.pdf

### 9. Federal Reserve Board: $15 billion a year moves from "naive" to "sophisticated" cardholders
- **Label:** VERIFIED. This is a primary Fed working paper: Agarwal, Presbitero, Silva and Wix, "Who Pays For Your Rewards? Redistribution in the Credit Card Market," FEDS 2023-007, January 2023. The authors' views are not official Fed policy.
- **Exact quotes from the abstract:**
  - "sophisticated individuals profit from reward credit cards at the expense of naive consumers"
  - "We estimate an aggregate annual redistribution of $15 billion"
- **Detail:** The money flows from less to more educated, from poorer to richer, and from high- to low-minority areas. Reward cards push people to spend more, and naive users end up carrying bigger unpaid balances.
- **Safe wording:** "Fed economists estimate rewards cards shift about $15 billion a year from people who carry balances to savvy users who don't."
- **URL:** https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/who-pays-for-your-rewards-redistribution-in-the-credit-card-market.htm

### 10. GAO: most of the fee goes to the bank that issued your card, and it helps pay for rewards
- **Label:** VERIFIED. This is a government document: GAO-10-45, November 2009.
- **Detail:**
  - In GAO's $100 example, the merchant pays $2.20 in fees. Of that, $1.70 is interchange that goes to the card-issuing bank.
  - Rewards programs "are funded in part by interchange fee revenues."
  - "Premium cards such as those offering rewards ... generally have higher rates than those for standard or traditional cards."
- **Exact quote:** "As a result all customers, including cash and check users, may pay higher prices for goods and services."
- **Safe wording:** "On a $100 purchase in GAO's example, $1.70 of the store's $2.20 card cost went to the bank that issued the card, and rewards cards cost stores more to accept."
- **URL:** https://www.gao.gov/assets/a298669.html (product page: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-10-45)

### 11. CFPB: more than $40 billion in rewards earned in 2022
- **Label:** VERIFIED. This is a government document: the CFPB circular as printed in the Federal Register, December 30, 2024.
- **Exact quotes:**
  - "consumers earned more than $40 billion in rewards from major general-purpose credit cards"
  - "by the end of 2022, 75 percent of general purpose credit cards were rewards cards"
  - "consumers also forfeit about $500 million in rewards each year"
- **Safe wording:** "Americans earned more than $40 billion in card rewards in 2022, and three in four general-purpose cards were rewards cards." Always say 2022: no newer total was found.
- **URL:** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-12-30/html/2024-30988.htm

### 12. Amex 2025: $37.4B in merchant fees, $18.4B spent on rewards

### 15. The Visa-Mastercard settlement would let stores turn down premium rewards cards
- **Label:** VERIFIED. Payments Dive reported it twice, on June 9, 2026 and October 16, 2026. The approval so far is only preliminary.
- **Detail:**
  - Judge Brian Cogan (E.D.N.Y.) gave preliminary approval on June 9, 2026 to a deal covering about 12 million merchants.
  - Credit interchange would fall 10 basis points for five years.
  - Standard consumer cards would get a 1.25% rate for eight years.
  - Merchants could decline some higher-cost premium and commercial cards, breaking the "honor all cards" rule, and would get new surcharging rights.
  - Walmart, NRF, the Retail Industry Leaders Association and other trade groups object.
- **Exact quote:** Judge Cogan: "None of the objectors have persuaded the Court that they will, or even can, get more through trial."
- **Safe wording:** "A proposed settlement, not yet final, would let stores refuse some premium rewards cards for the first time."

## Common myths

1. **MYTH: "Rewards are free money from the bank."**
   - **What is true:** Rewards are funded in part by the interchange fees merchants pay the issuing bank (GAO-10-45). Merchants pass those fees on in their prices.
   - Amex spent $18.4B on rewards in 2025 while collecting $37.4B in merchant fees.
2. **MYTH: "If I pay cash, swipe fees don't touch me."**
   - **What is true:** Stores generally charge one price to everyone. The Boston Fed estimates each household that doesn't pay by credit card transfers about $149 a year to card users.
   - The same paper says about 79% of banks' merchant-fee revenue comes from cash payers.
3. **MYTH: "The swipe fee goes to Visa and Mastercard."**
   - **What is true:** The networks set the rates, but the largest part is interchange, which goes to the bank that issued the card. In GAO's example, $1.70 of a $2.20 fee went to the issuer.
4. **MYTH: "Only poor people lose; anyone with a rewards card wins."**
   - **What is true:** Fed Board economists found about $15B a year flows from "naive" cardholders to "sophisticated" ones, regardless of income. Rewards-card users who carry balances help pay for the points of people who pay in full.
5. **MYTH: "Swipe fees were $236 billion" stated as fact.**
   - **What is true:** That is a 2024 estimate by CMSPI, a consultancy that works for merchants. The most-cited count (Nilson) was $187.2B for 2024 and about $198B for 2025.

---

## Strongest hook number
**$1,133.** That is what the average credit-card-using household gets each year from people who pay another way, according to a Boston Fed study. The cash payer's side is $149 a year.

Alternative hook: "$198 billion in swipe fees in 2025."

## Strongest twist
The person paying for your free flight is the shopper behind you paying cash. Stores price card fees into everyone's bill. In the Boston Fed's estimate, the lowest-income households pay about $21 a year into the system while the highest-income collect about $750.

Even among cardholders, Fed economists found about $15B a year moves from people who carry balances to people who pay in full.

## Could change soon
- **Visa-Mastercard settlement:** The final approval hearing before Judge Cogan is on November 16, 2026. Merchant groups have vowed to appeal, so the premium-card-refusal and 10-basis-point cut terms are not final.
- **Debit fee cap:** The Fed's debit-fee cap (Regulation II) is under appeal at the Eighth Circuit after an August 2025 district-court ruling vacated it.
- **Credit Card Competition Act:** The bill is still pending in Congress.
