# Diamonds — facts (Markup: how a business really makes its money)

Researched 2026-10-23. Every URL below was opened. Labels: VERIFIED (primary source, or two reliable sources), REPORTED (one reliable source), CAREFUL (true only in the wording given), MYTH (popular but wrong).

## Notes on the lead facts

- "A Diamond Is Forever" written in 1947 for De Beers: CAREFUL. Most sources, including coverage of De Beers' own 2026 centenary book, say 1947. Some say the line first ran in ads in 1948. Use "written in 1947".
- Lab-grown prices fell "about 90% at wholesale since 2018": CAREFUL. That is De Beers' own estimate from its May 2025 Lightbox release. A newer independent index (Edahn Golan, via JCK, August 2026) puts the drop at 96%. The 2018 Reuters story given as a source is from December 2018 and does not contain the 90% figure.
- De Beers "closed" Lightbox in 2025: CAREFUL. De Beers *announced* the closure on 8–9 May 2025. I found no source confirming the date the site went dark or that an asset sale was completed. Use "announced it would shut Lightbox in May 2025".
- Both supplied source pages (streetinsider.com, rapaport.com) returned 403. I used syndicated copies and other outlets instead.

## Facts

1. **The slogan and its writer.** CAREFUL
   Safe wording: "In 1947, a copywriter named Frances Gerety at the Philadelphia ad agency N.W. Ayer wrote four words for De Beers: 'A Diamond Is Forever.' In 1999, Advertising Age named it the slogan of the 20th century."
   Gerety never married (NPR/WBUR interview with novelist J. Courtney Sullivan). Ayer had been founded in Philadelphia in 1869.
   Do not use the "late-night, nightgown and curlers" details. They come from Sullivan's novel, not from the historical record.

2. **The ad agency's mission.** REPORTED
   Safe wording: "De Beers hired N.W. Ayer in 1938. The New York Times reported that the agency's aim was to 'create a situation where almost every person pledging marriage feels compelled to acquire a diamond engagement ring.'"
   The quote comes from the NYT as cited by PhillyVoice. Al Jazeera gives 1946 as the hiring year, so the date is disputed; keep it to "late 1930s".
   URL: https://www.phillyvoice.com/how-philly-ad-agency-standardized-diamond-engagement-ring/

3. **How the custom took off.** CAREFUL
   Safe wording: "Before World War II, only about 1 in 10 American engagement rings had a diamond. By the late 20th century it was about 8 in 10."

   URLs: https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2025/9/14/how-the-diamond-engagement-ring-was-invented-and-sold-around-the-world · https://www.phillyvoice.com/how-philly-ad-agency-standardized-diamond-engagement-ring/

## Most common myths

1. **MYTH: "Diamond engagement rings are an age-old tradition."**
   True: it became a mass custom in the 20th century. Before WWII only about 1 in 10 US engagement rings had a diamond; by the late 20th century about 8 in 10 did, after De Beers' ad campaign.
   (Al Jazeera; PhillyVoice, URLs above)

2. **MYTH: "You're supposed to spend two months' salary."**
   True: that guideline came from De Beers ads. They began with one month's salary and moved to two months in the 1980s.
   (Al Jazeera; MediaBrief, URLs above)

3. **MYTH: "Diamonds are expensive because they're rare."**
   True: supply was managed. De Beers once controlled about 90% of world supply (REPORTED, Al Jazeera), which the outlet calls "an illusion of diamond scarcity." De Beers alone mined 21.7 million carats in just nine months of 2026 (Anglo American).

4. **MYTH: "A diamond holds its value."**
   True: resale typically fetches only about 50% of the original retail price (REPORTED, Al Jazeera). De Beers' own average rough price fell 37% in a year (Anglo American, Q3 2026). One New York dealer told JCK: "There is no resale value for lab-grown diamonds."

5. **MYTH: "Lab-grown diamonds are fake."**
   True: the FTC says they are diamonds, with essentially the same optical, physical and chemical properties. They just must be labeled lab-grown.
   (FTC; National Jeweler, URLs above)

## Strongest hook number

**96%**: how far lab-grown diamond wholesale prices have fallen since 2018 on Golan's index (JCK, Aug 2026). If you need the company's own number, De Beers itself said 90%.
Visual pairing: Lightbox launched at $800 a carat in 2018; by 2025 De Beers' CEO said you could buy lab-grown "in supermarkets for $200."

## Strongest twist

The company that taught the world "A Diamond Is Forever" launched its own lab-grown line in 2018, calling it jewelry that "may not be forever, but is perfect for right now." Seven years later it announced it would kill that brand to protect the natural-diamond story. Now De Beers itself is being sold for a reported ~$1 billion, after about $6.8 billion in write-downs.

## Could change soon

The De Beers sale could be signed any week. Anglo and Botswana both point to Q4 2026, but nothing is signed yet; Botswana could use its pre-emption right, and Richemont or Signet joining is unconfirmed. The ~$1 billion price is not final, and lab-grown and De Beers rough prices update every quarter, so re-check all of this before publishing.
