# Popcorn — Facts (Markup: how a business really makes its money)

Researched 2026-10-23. Newest full-year figures: fiscal 2025 (AMC and Cinemark both report on calendar years; FY2026 results are not out yet).
Labels: VERIFIED = primary source or two reliable sources. REPORTED = one reliable source. CAREFUL = true only in the exact wording given. MYTH = popular but wrong.

## Facts

1. **AMC food and drink: $1,671.3M revenue, $327.0M cost (2025).** VERIFIED (primary, SEC filing).
   Safe wording: "In 2025 AMC sold $1.67 billion of food and drink. The food and drink itself cost it $327 million, about 20 cents of every dollar."
   Note: the 19.6% is calculated from the two reported lines; the release does not print a percentage. The cost line covers ingredients and supplies, not staff wages or rent.
   URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1411579/000141157926000018/amc-20260223xex99d1.htm

2. **AMC tickets: $2,652.8M admissions revenue, $1,275.2M film exhibition costs (2025).** VERIFIED (primary, SEC filing).
   Safe wording: "Of every ticket dollar AMC took in last year, about 48 cents went back to the studios."
   Note: 48.1% is calculated from the reported lines. The 10-K says film costs "are based on a share of admissions revenues."
   URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1411579/000141157926000018/amc-20260223xex99d1.htm

3. **AMC per visitor (2025): $7.62 food and drink vs $12.09 ticket worldwide; $8.57 vs $12.78 in the U.S.** VERIFIED (primary, SEC filing).
   Safe wording: "At AMC's U.S. theaters, the average visitor spent $12.78 on a ticket and another $8.57 on snacks and drinks in 2025."
   CAREFUL: $7.62 / $12.09 are consolidated figures that include Europe ($5.28 snacks / $10.39 tickets). For a US audience, use $8.57 / $12.78.
   URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1411579/000141157926000018/amc-20260223xex99d1.htm

4. **After direct costs, AMC's snack counter earned about as much as its box office (2025).** CAREFUL (my arithmetic from the primary filing).
   Math: tickets $2,652.8M minus $1,275.2M film costs = $1,377.6M. Food and drink $1,671.3M minus $327.0M = $1,344.3M. About $6.28 per visitor from the ticket and $6.13 from snacks.
   Safe wording: "After paying the studios and paying for the food, AMC kept about $1.38 billion from tickets and about $1.34 billion from snacks. Nearly the same amount." Always say "before wages and rent" and do not call it "profit."
   URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1411579/000141157926000018/amc-20260223xex99d1.htm

5. **Cinemark shows the same pattern (2025).** VERIFIED (primary, company release filed with SEC).
   Concession revenue $1,227.2M, concession supplies $240.5M (19.6%). Film rentals and advertising $877.0M on $1,544.7M admissions (56.8%). U.S. per visitor: $8.30 concessions vs $10.52 ticket. CEO Sean Gamble cited "all-time high concession sales and per caps." Release dated February 18, 2026.
   Safe wording: "Cinemark, the number-three U.S. chain, paid about 20 cents in supplies for every concession dollar, and more than half of every ticket dollar to studios and advertising." (Leave "number-three" out unless you verify it separately. Safer: "Cinemark, another big U.S. chain.")
   URL: https://ir.cinemark.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001193125-26-056010/cnk-ex99_1.htm

6. **On the biggest films, studios take even more. Disney asked about 65% of ticket revenue for "Star Wars: The Last Jedi" (2017).** VERIFIED (Dow Jones Newswires and Manatt).
   Details: theaters had to play it in their largest auditorium for at least four weeks, and Disney could take another 5% (about 70%) if a theater broke any condition. Dow Jones: "Most theatrical releases send about 55% of ticket sales back to studios."
   Safe wording: "For 'The Last Jedi,' Disney reportedly asked for about 65 percent of every ticket."
   URLs: https://foxbusiness.com/features/disney-lays-down-the-law-for-theaters-on-star-wars-the-last-jedi-update and https://www.manatt.com/insights/news/2017/disney-sets-strict-release-terms-for-new-star-wars

7. **Economists argue expensive popcorn is what keeps tickets cheap.** REPORTED (Stanford GSB write-up of the study, December 1, 2009).
   Stanford marketing professor Wesley Hartmann and UC Santa Cruz economist Ricard Gil studied revenue data from a chain of theaters in Spain. Hartmann: "If you want to bring more consumers into the market, you need to keep ticket prices lower to attract them." The write-up also says concessions are "only about 20 percent of gross revenues" but about 40 percent of theaters' profits.
   CAREFUL: the 20% / 40% split is a 2009 estimate. Today concessions are about a third of AMC's revenue (34%, from fact 1 and total revenue of $4,848.9M). Do not present 20/40 as current.
   URL: https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/why-does-movie-popcorn-cost-so-much?page_id=39

8. **Theaters originally wanted nothing to do with popcorn.** VERIFIED (Smithsonian and NPR, both quoting historian Andrew F. Smith, author of "Popped Culture").
   Smith (Smithsonian): "Movie theaters wanted nothing to do with popcorn." Also "They had beautiful carpets and rugs and didn't want popcorn being ground into it." Smith (NPR, 2025): "They had rugs on the floor and they didn't really want people coming in and eating things."
   Drawable: ornate 1910s-20s movie palaces with grand ceilings and rugs, and street vendors with popping machines outside. Smithsonian: some early theaters "had signs hung outside their coatrooms" asking patrons to check their popcorn with their coats (REPORTED, Smithsonian only).
   URLs: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/why-do-we-eat-popcorn-at-the-movies-475063/ and https://www.ctpublic.org/2025-01-19/on-national-popcorn-day-we-look-back-at-how-they-became-a-movie-theater-staple

   URLs: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/why-do-we-eat-popcorn-at-the-movies-475063/ and https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/05/25/heres-why-we-eat-popcorn-at-the-movies/

## Common myths

1. **MYTH: "Theaters make nothing on tickets. The studios take it all."**
   True: AMC kept about 52 cents of every ticket dollar in 2025, after film costs of 48.1% (fact 2). Cinemark kept about 43 cents after film rentals and advertising (fact 5). Studios take bigger shares on the biggest releases, around 65% for "The Last Jedi" (fact 6), but not "all of it."

2. **MYTH: "Popcorn is 90%+ pure profit."**
   True: at AMC and Cinemark, food and drink supplies cost about 20% of food and drink sales in 2025 (facts 1 and 5). That figure covers all food and drink, not popcorn alone, and it does not count staff, rent or equipment. Say "the food costs about 20 cents per dollar," not "90% profit." A Wedbush analyst told CNN "Over 80% of that revenue is going to profit," but that describes margin over food cost, not real profit (https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/05/25/heres-why-we-eat-popcorn-at-the-movies/).

3. **MYTH: "Popcorn and movies have always gone together."**
   True: early movie palaces kept popcorn out to protect their carpets. It moved inside during the Great Depression, when a 5- or 10-cent bag kept theaters afloat (facts 8 and 9).

4. **MYTH: "High popcorn prices are pure greed on top of high ticket prices."**
   True: the Hartmann and Gil research argues the reverse. Theaters charge more for snacks so they can keep tickets cheaper and fill seats with price-sensitive moviegoers (fact 7). This is an economists' explanation, not a confession from the theaters.

5. **MYTH: "Popcorn was served at the first Thanksgiving."**
   True: according to historian Andrew F. Smith, quoted by CNN, popcorn likely reached New England in the early 19th century and was not a "first Thanksgiving" dish. REPORTED (one source). URL: https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/05/25/heres-why-we-eat-popcorn-at-the-movies/

## Strongest hook number
**About 20 cents.** For every dollar of popcorn and soda AMC sold in 2025, the food and drink cost it about 20 cents ($327.0M on $1,671.3M). For every ticket dollar, about 48 cents went to the studios.
Alternate: the average U.S. AMC visitor spent $8.57 on snacks on top of a $12.78 ticket.

## Strongest twist
After paying the studios and paying for the food, AMC's snack counter brought in almost exactly as much as its entire box office: about $1.34B vs $1.38B in 2025 (fact 4, before wages and rent). The snack theaters once banned is now worth as much to them as the movies, and economists argue it is the reason tickets aren't even more expensive.
