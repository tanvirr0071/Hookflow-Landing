# Costco's $1.50 hot dog: facts

Series: Markup. Research date: 2026-10-23. Newest full year: **fiscal 2026** (52 weeks ended Aug 30, 2026; results released Sept 24, 2026). The fiscal 2025 figures given in the brief are now one year old. The newest annual hot dog sales count is still **fiscal 2025**, because the FY2026 call did not give one.

## Facts

1. **VERIFIED: fiscal 2026 membership fees were $5.907B**, up from $5.323B in FY2025, an 11% rise.
   - Safe wording: "Last year Costco collected about $5.9 billion just in membership fees."
   - Sources: Costco Q4/FY2026 release, Sept 24, 2026: https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_news/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2026-Operating-Results-2026.pdf · FY2026 10-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000909832/000090983226000093/cost-20260830.htm

2. **VERIFIED: fiscal 2026 net income was $9.226B ($20.76 per diluted share).** FY2025 was $8.099B ($18.21).
   - Q4 included a non-recurring benefit of $0.15/share from IEEPA tariff refunds.
   - Net sales were $297.2B and total revenue $303.2B.
   - Safe wording: "Costco made about $9.2 billion in profit last year on nearly $300 billion in sales."
   - Sources: FY2026 release (above) · FY2025 release: https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2025/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Operating-Results/default.aspx

3. **CAREFUL: membership fees vs profit (my arithmetic on Costco's FY2026 income statement).**
   - Fees of $5.907B equal about **64% of net income** ($9.226B) and about **half of operating income** ($11.685B).
   - Operating income minus membership fees leaves $5.778B. That is less than the $5.907B in fees, so on paper the fees outweigh the operating profit from selling $297B of goods.
   - Safe wording: "Membership fees alone add up to about half of Costco's operating profit." Do NOT say "all of Costco's profit is membership fees." Costco does not break out the costs of running membership, and net income is after tax while fees are before tax.
   - Source: FY2026 release, income statement (p.3): https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_news/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2026-Operating-Results-2026.pdf

4. **VERIFIED: thin margin on goods.** FY2026 gross margin was **11.09%** of net sales, down 3 basis points from 11.12%.
   - Merchandise costs were $264.3B against $297.2B in net sales.
   - Safe wording: "On every $100 of stuff, Costco keeps about $11 before paying its workers, rent and bills."
   - Source: FY2026 10-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000909832/000090983226000093/cost-20260830.htm

5. **VERIFIED: 84.1 million paid members at FY2026 year end**, including 42.3 million Executive members; 150.4 million cardholders. Renewal rate: **92.3% in the U.S. and Canada**, 89.8% worldwide.
   - Safe wording: "More than 9 in 10 U.S. and Canadian members renew."
   - Source: FY2026 10-K (above) · Q4 FY2026 call, CFO Gary Millerchip, Sept 24, 2026: https://www.webull.com/news/15660435456459776

6. **VERIFIED: the fee went up on Sept 1, 2024**: basic (Gold Star) from $60 to $65, Executive from $120 to $130.
   - The fee-hike share of membership income growth was about 30% in FY2026 and 40% in FY2025, per the 10-K.
   - Safe wording: "The $65 card went up in 2024. The $1.50 hot dog didn't."
   - Sources: FY2026 10-K ("we increased annual membership fees in the U.S. and Canada, effective September 1, 2024") · Scripps News: https://www.scrippsnews.com/business/company-news/costcos-1-50-hot-dog-combo-faces-inflation-the-ceos-answer

7. **VERIFIED: Costco sold over 245 million hot dog combos in fiscal 2025.** The shareholder report says 245.1 million; it also lists 157.4 million rotisserie chickens.
   - CFO Gary Millerchip, FY2025 Q4 call (Sept 25, 2025): "we sold over 245 million hot dog combos, over 157 million rotisserie chickens."
   - Safe wording: "Costco sold more than 245 million hot dog combos in a single year."
   - Sources: CFO Dive: https://www.cfodive.com/news/costco-cfo-calls-hot-dog-combo-important-amid-economic-uncertainty/761297/ · Chowhound, citing Costco's 2026 Annual Shareholders Meeting deck: https://www.chowhound.com/2094755/costco-hot-dog-soda-combos-sold-2025

8. **VERIFIED: sales have grown from about 135 million hot dogs a year (reported in 2018) to 245 million combos (FY2025).**
   - Safe wording: "In 2018 Costco was selling about 135 million hot dogs a year. By 2025 it was over 245 million." Do not call it an exact percentage: the 2018 figure was an "average".
   - Sources: 425Business, Apr 18, 2018: https://www.425business.com/news/costco-ceo-craig-jelinek-on-shareholders-costco-com-and-hot-dogs/article_5ff4b632-1f75-5e98-b9ff-6e02d676668b.html · CFO Dive (above)

9. **CAREFUL: total combo revenue is small next to fees.** 245.1M × $1.50 ≈ **$368M**, about 6% of one year's membership fees.
   - This is my estimate. Prices differ outside the U.S. and Costco does not report hot dog revenue.
   - Safe wording: "Even at 245 million combos, that's roughly a few hundred million dollars, a rounding error next to $5.9 billion in membership fees."
   - Sources: CFO Dive (above) · FY2026 release (above)

10. **CAREFUL: the $1.50 price dates to the mid-1980s.** Costco and most major outlets say 1985. The CFO marked the combo's "40th anniversary" in 2025. Some outlets (Mental Floss, Tasting Table) say the hot dog arrived in 1984.
    - Safe wording: "$1.50 since the mid-'80s", or "since 1985, by Costco's count."
    - Sources: CFO Dive (above) · ABC7 / ABC News, May 31, 2024: https://abc7ny.com/post/costcos-150-hot-dog-price-safe/14895838/ · Mental Floss: https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/547020/costco-150-hot-dog-soda-combo-enigma

11. **VERIFIED (quote): Jim Sinegal to Craig Jelinek.** Jelinek, then CEO, told an Issaquah Chamber of Commerce luncheon on April 12, 2018 what he had said: "I came to (Jim Sinegal) once and I said, 'Jim, we can't sell this hot dog for a buck fifty. We are losing our rear ends.'"
    - Sinegal's reply: **"If you raise the effing hot dog, I will kill you. Figure it out."**
    - Safe wording: "Costco's co-founder told his successor: 'If you raise the effing hot dog, I will kill you. Figure it out.'" This is Jelinek's retelling, not a recording of Sinegal.
    - Sources: 425Business (above) · The Ringer, Alan Siegel, July 2, 2026: https://theringer.com/2026/07/02/food/costco-hot-dog-history-explained-cheap

12. **VERIFIED (quote): CEO Ron Vachris, Costco Instagram video, March 2026** (The Ringer gives March 18; 41 seconds long): **"The hot dog price will not change as long as I'm around."**
    - Visual: he orders a **plain** hot dog with no toppings, sits at a food court table and takes a bite.
    - Caption: "When you run the company but you still know the best lunch in town only costs $1.50."
    - The Ringer reports nearly 9 million views in a day. Vachris started at Costco as a forklift driver.
    - Sources: Newsweek, Mar 20, 2026: https://www.newsweek.com/costco-ceo-reveals-if-price-hot-dog-changing-11708370 · The Ringer (above) · Scripps News (above)

13. **VERIFIED (quote): CFO Gary Millerchip, earnings call, May 30, 2024** (his first call as CFO): "To clear up some recent media speculation, I also want to confirm the $1.50 hot dog price is safe."
    - Source: ABC7 / ABC News: https://abc7ny.com/post/costcos-150-hot-dog-price-safe/14895838/

14. **VERIFIED: Costco makes its own hot dogs.** Instead of raising the price, it stopped buying Hebrew National franks and built a Kirkland Signature hot dog plant in Los Angeles, then a second near Chicago (Morris, Ill.).
    - The switch was around 2008–2009; sources differ on the year.
    - Food court director Bob Collins says the Kirkland frank is "about 9 percent larger than the original."
    - Safe wording: "So Costco built its own hot dog factories."
    - Sources: 425Business (above) · Mental Floss (above) · The Ringer (above)

15. **REPORTED: what to draw.**
    - The original dogs were Hebrew National franks sold from carts that Price Club (Costco's 1993 merger partner) bought "left over from the 1984 Olympics in Los Angeles."
    - Those carts stood outside Price Club's first store on Morena Boulevard, San Diego.
    - The early snack bar was called "Cafe 150", named for the $1.50 price.
    - The combo today: a quarter-pound all-beef dog plus a 20-oz fountain soda. In 2026 Costco added a bottled-water option.
    - Food courts keep umbrellas up "even in indoor food courts" (Collins).
    - Soda fountains went back from Pepsi (used since 2013) to **Coca-Cola** in 2025, as Vachris announced at the January 2025 shareholders meeting.
    - Sources: The Ringer (above) · Mental Floss (above) · FOX 5 NY, Apr 5, 2025: https://www.fox5ny.com/news/costco-food-court-changes

## Myths vs truth

1. **MYTH: "The hot dog is how Costco makes money."** It is a draw. Costco makes its money on the **$5.9B in membership fees** (about half of operating income). Combo revenue, roughly $368M even at 245M combos, is small by comparison.
2. **MYTH: "Costco has never changed anything about the combo."** Several things have changed:
   - The frank switched from Hebrew National to in-house Kirkland Signature (about 9% larger).
   - The soda went from Coke to Pepsi (2013), then back to Coke (2025).
   - A water option was added in 2026.
   - The Polish dog was dropped.
   - Only the $1.50 price has held.
3. **MYTH: "Everything at the food court is frozen in time."** Other items went up: the soda alone went from 59¢ to 69¢ (2022) and then 79¢ (2025), and the chicken bake went from $2.99 to $3.99 (2025), per Scripps News. Only the combo stayed at $1.50.
4. **CAREFUL: "Costco loses money on every hot dog."** Costco has never published per-hot-dog profit. The only on-record statement is Jelinek recalling that, years ago, "we are losing our rear ends", which came before Costco built its own plants. Use: "Costco has suggested it makes little or nothing on it."
5. **MYTH: "The $1.50 price is company policy."** It is a promise by the leadership ("as long as I'm around"). It is not a written policy or contract.

## Hook and twist

- **Strongest hook number:** **245 million.** "Costco sold more than 245 million $1.50 hot dog combos last year, and it didn't need to make a cent on them."
- **Strongest twist:** The money is in the **$65 card**, not the **$1.50 dog**. Costco raised the membership fee in 2024 but not the hot dog. Fees brought in **$5.9 billion** in FY2026, about half of all operating profit, and 92.3% of U.S./Canada members renewed. The hot dog is the ad that keeps them renewing.

## Could change soon

The $1.50 price rests on CEO Ron Vachris's personal pledge, not a policy. The membership fee was last raised in Sept 2024, and the next fiscal 2027 quarterly results will update the membership and fee figures above.
