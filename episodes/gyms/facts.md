## Facts (15)

### 1. Planet Fitness today: about 20.8 million members and 2,896 clubs (end of 2025). VERIFIED
- 10-K: "We ended the year with approximately 20.8 million members and 2,896 clubs". The FY2025 release gives the same numbers and says about 1.1 million net new members joined in 2025.
- Split: 2,604 franchisee-owned and 292 corporate-owned clubs. Clubs are in all 50 states, DC, Puerto Rico, Canada, Panama, Mexico, Australia and Spain.
- Safe wording: "At the end of 2025, Planet Fitness had about 20.8 million members in 2,896 gyms."
- URLs: [10-K], [Q4-25]

### 2. On average that is about 7,200 members per gym. CAREFUL
- This is our own division of the filing numbers: 20.8 million ÷ 2,896 = about 7,180. The company does not publish a members-per-club figure, and the average hides wide variation between clubs.
- Safe wording: "That works out to roughly 7,200 members for every Planet Fitness, on average."
- URL: [10-K]

### 3. The 2014 NPR figure: 6,500 members per gym, room for about 300. CAREFUL
- NPR: "Planet Fitness, which charges between $10 and $20 per month, has, on average, 6,500 members per gym." Also: "Most of its gyms can hold around 300 people."
- The 6,500 is a chain-wide average. It is not a count for one gym. When NPR asked the manager of the Midtown Manhattan club, Chrysanthi Laskaris, how many members it had, she said "About 6,000." Asked how many it could hold, she said "I'd say a few hundred, to be completely honest."
- Safe wording: "In 2014, NPR reported Planet Fitness averaged 6,500 members per gym, and most of its gyms could hold around 300 people at once."
- URL: [NPR]

### 4. The manager's line about full turnout. REPORTED
- Planet Fitness Midtown Manhattan manager Chrysanthi Laskaris to NPR (2014), on what would happen if every member came at once: "Thank goodness they all don't show up at one time."
- Safe wording: quote it exactly and attribute it to the manager of one Manhattan Planet Fitness, speaking to NPR in 2014.
- URL: [NPR]

### 5. Who pays for whom. REPORTED
- NPR's explanation of the model is that members who don't go subsidize the members who do, which keeps the price low for everyone. NPR also reported that "most lose around half their members every year" (about gyms in general).
- Safe wording: "NPR's Planet Money put it simply: the people who don't show up help pay for the people who do."
- URL: [NPR]

### 6. Around 60% of members did not visit in a 30-day stretch (2022). REPORTED
- CNN (2022): "Planet Fitness members typically go five or six times a month, and around 60% of them don't visit in a 30-day stretch." This is one outlet, it is from 2022, and CNN gives no further attribution. Do not present it as current or as a company filing.
- Safe wording: "In 2022, CNN reported that around 60% of Planet Fitness members didn't visit in a given 30-day stretch."
- URL: [CNN22]

### 7. The price is set so you won't cancel. REPORTED
- BMO Capital Markets analyst Simeon Siegel told CNN the $10 price was "a very powerful marketing tool". CNN paraphrased his argument: the price is cheap enough to attract people and low enough that members who skip workouts don't cancel.
- Then-CEO Chris Rondeau, also to CNN: "It's a 'get you off the couch' price." And: "The longer I can stay at $10 the better off I am to get people off the couch."
- Safe wording: quote exactly and attribute to the speaker, CNN, November 2022.
- URL: [CNN22]

### 8. The $10 price lasted from 1998 to 2024, then rose to $15 for new members. VERIFIED
- CNN (May 2024): Planet Fitness "will raise the price of its 'classic' membership from $10 a month to $15 for new members", which raised the basic plan by 50% "for the first time since 1998". The FY2025 10-K confirms Classic Card dues "starting at only $15 per month to new members".
- Then-interim CEO Craig Benson: "We've seen in every industry people move in price".
- Safe wording: "The $10 plan lasted from 1998 until 2024. New members now pay from $15 a month."
- URLs: [CNN24], [10-K]

### 9. The cheap plan is not what most members pay: two-thirds are on the $24.99 Black Card. VERIFIED (primary)
- 10-K: PF Black Card is "$24.99 per month for new members". Black Card penetration was "66.5% as of December 31, 2025". There is also a standard annual fee: "current standard annual fees of $49."
- Black Card extras listed in the 10-K: "water massage beds and chairs, massage chairs, tanning equipment, hot/cold recovery lounges", a guest on each visit, and access to any club.
- CNN (2022) said "The average Planet Fitness member pays around $17.60 a month."
- Safe wording: "About two-thirds of Planet Fitness members pay $24.99 a month for the Black Card, plus a $49 yearly fee."
- URLs: [10-K], [CNN22]

### 10. It is a dues machine: more than 90% recurring revenue, pulled automatically. VERIFIED (primary)
- 10-K: "over 90% of both our corporate-owned club and franchise revenues consisted of recurring revenue streams". Dues and annual fees are collected by electronic funds transfer: "We utilize electronic funds transfer ('EFT') as our primary method of collecting monthly dues and annual membership fees."
- FY2025 money: total revenue $1,324.1 million, up 12.1%. Net income was $220.3 million. System-wide sales were $5.3 billion.
- Safe wording: "In 2025, Planet Fitness took in $1.32 billion in revenue, and over 90% of its club and franchise revenue was recurring. Monthly dues are pulled automatically by electronic funds transfer."
- URLs: [10-K], [Q4-25]

### 11. It also sells the treadmills: $310 million from equipment in 2025. VERIFIED (primary)
- FY2025 segment revenue: franchise $468.0 million, corporate-owned clubs $546.1 million, equipment $310.1 million. The equipment segment sells machines to franchisee-owned clubs. About 90% of clubs are run by franchisees.
- Safe wording: "Almost a quarter of Planet Fitness's 2025 revenue, $310 million, came from selling gym equipment to its own franchisees."
- URL: [Q4-25]

### 12. The study: members paying over $70 a month went 4.3 times a month, more than $17 a visit. VERIFIED (peer-reviewed, primary)
- DellaVigna and Malmendier tracked 7,752 members of three U.S. health clubs (New England, Boston area) over three years, roughly 1997-2001. Abstract: "members who choose a contract with a flat monthly fee of over $70 attend on average 4.3 times per month. They pay a price per expected visit of more than $17, even though they could pay $10 per visit using a 10-visit pass. On average, these users forgo savings of $600 during their membership."
- Also: "Eighty percent of the monthly members would have been better off had they paid per visit for the same number of visits."
- Safe wording: "In a study of 7,752 gym members, people on $70-plus monthly plans went about 4 times a month, more than $17 per visit, when a 10-visit pass cost $10 a visit."
- URL: [DM]

### 13. People wait more than two months after their last visit to cancel. VERIFIED (peer-reviewed, primary)
- DellaVigna and Malmendier, Table 1, Finding 4: the "Interval between last attendance and termination" was 2.31 full months. Finding 5: monthly members were "17 percent more likely to stay enrolled beyond one year" than annual members, even though they "pay higher fees for the option to cancel each month."
- Safe wording: "In the study, members typically kept paying for more than two months after their last workout before cancelling."
- URL: [DM]

### 14. What the study's gyms looked like at check-in (drawable). VERIFIED (primary)
- From the paper (clubs in the study, late 1990s): "users receive cards they have to deposit in a basket at the front desk when they enter. While they are exercising, a health club employee swipes them... and users pick them up when they exit." The contracts included "a temporary locker, towels, and access to the equipment." Members could "freeze" a membership for three months a year. The average member was in their early thirties at sign-up.
- Safe wording: "In the study, members dropped a card in a basket at the front desk, and staff swiped it while they worked out."
- URL: [DM]

### 15. What a Planet Fitness looks like (drawable). VERIFIED (company sources)
- 10-K: clubs are "typically 20,000 square feet", bright and clean, packed with cardio and strength machines, with no group classes, pools, day care or juice bars. Brand line: "Planet Fitness is the home of the Judgement Free Zone". The company says it has "not needed to impose time limits on our cardio machines". Members are about 51% male, and Gen Z is the "fastest growing demographic".
- Company press release (2015): the "Lunk Alarm" is "a purple and yellow siren on the wall used as a light-hearted, gentle reminder" that grunting, dropping weights or judging others is not allowed. There was free pizza on "the first Monday of every month" and free bagels on the second Tuesday, "while supplies last". NPR (2014) also reported monthly bagel breakfasts, pizza dinners and massage chairs. CNN (2022) also reports the "lunk alarm".
- Then-CEO Chris Rondeau to Entrepreneur (2020): "We don't have pools, we don't have classes, we don't have instructors to pay." Also: "We run our stores with 15 people." And: "Forty percent of our members had never belonged to a gym before joining Planet Fitness."
- Safe wording: draw a large, bright room of rows of treadmills and machines with a purple and yellow siren (the Lunk Alarm) on the wall. Do not claim the walls themselves are purple and yellow; no source opened says so. Treat pizza and bagel nights as "has offered", not as current policy at every club, because the release is from 2015.
- URLs: [10-K], [PR15], [NPR], [CNN22], [ENT]

## Myths and what is true instead

1. MYTH: "One Planet Fitness had 6,500 members and room for 300."
   TRUE: NPR's 6,500 is the chain-wide average per gym in 2014. The Manhattan manager NPR spoke to estimated "About 6,000" for her club and capacity of "a few hundred". Today the average is about 7,200 members per club (20.8M ÷ 2,896). [NPR], [10-K]

2. MYTH: "Planet Fitness is $10 a month."
   TRUE: New members have paid from $15 for the Classic Card since 2024, plus a $49 annual fee. 66.5% of members are on the $24.99 Black Card. [CNN24], [10-K]

3. MYTH: "Planet Fitness admits its business depends on people not showing up."
   TRUE: That is how journalists and analysts (NPR, CNN, BMO's Simeon Siegel) explain the model. Planet Fitness's own filings say no such thing. They pitch first-timers and a "Judgement Free Zone". Attribute any "relies on no-shows" line to the reporter or analyst, not the company. [NPR], [CNN22], [10-K]

4. MYTH: "Most gym members never go at all."
   TRUE: The data says people go less than they planned, not never. In the AER study, members on $70-plus plans averaged 4.3 visits a month. CNN (2022) said Planet Fitness members "typically go five or six times a month", while around 60% don't visit in a given 30-day stretch. So many skip months; it does not show that most never go. [DM], [CNN22]

5. MYTH: "Gyms make all their money from your dues."
   TRUE: At Planet Fitness, about 90% of clubs are franchised. The company earns royalties and fees from franchisees ($468.0M in 2025) and sells them equipment ($310.1M in 2025), alongside dues from its own 292 corporate clubs ($546.1M). [Q4-25], [10-K]

## Strongest hook number
About 7,200 members per gym, against the room for about 300 people at once that NPR reported for most gyms in 2014. Use it as: "Planet Fitness has about 20.8 million members and 2,896 gyms, roughly 7,200 members per gym." Label it CAREFUL, because it is our own division of the 10-K numbers. The 300-capacity figure is NPR's 2014 number, not a current filing.
Backup hook: "Around 60% of Planet Fitness members didn't visit in a 30-day stretch" (CNN, 2022).

## Strongest twist
The cheap price is not the product. Two-thirds of members (66.5%) pay $24.99 for the Black Card. And the low price works because it is too small to bother cancelling: members in the AER study kept paying for 2.31 months after their last visit, and monthly members were 17% more likely than annual members to still be enrolled after a year, even though they had paid extra for the right to quit any month.

## Could change soon
Planet Fitness cut its 2026 same-club sales outlook from 4-5% (February) to about 1% (reaffirmed 2026-08-06). CEO Colleen Keating said it has "initiated and expanded tests around pricing, member experience, and retention", so the $15 / $24.99 prices and the member count (Q3 2026 results are next) could change soon.
