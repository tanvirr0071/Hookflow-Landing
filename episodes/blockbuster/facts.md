## Facts (15)

### 1. The meeting: September 2000, Dallas, 23rd floor — VERIFIED (date and city) / REPORTED (floor)
Reed Hastings, Marc Randolph and CFO Barry McCarthy flew to Blockbuster's headquarters in Dallas. They met CEO John Antioco and general counsel Ed Stead. Randolph's own excerpt places the meeting "that morning in September of 2000". It says the elevator "opened onto the 23rd floor" of the Renaissance Tower, which he calls "an unbroken cube of steel and glass."
- **Safe wording:** "In September 2000, Netflix's founders flew to Blockbuster's Dallas headquarters to pitch a deal."
- Randolph excerpt (Built In, May 4, 2022, from *That Will Never Work*): https://builtin.com/articles/netflix-blockbuster-buyout
- The Independent via Yahoo News (Feb 7, 2022): https://www.yahoo.com/news/blockbuster-ceo-struggled-not-laugh-191553438.html

### 2. "Fifty million." — VERIFIED
In Randolph's account, Ed Stead asked: "If we were to buy you, what are you thinking?" Barry McCarthy began a longer answer, and Hastings cut in with "Fifty million." The pitch was a merger. Netflix would run the online business and Blockbuster would focus on its stores.
- **Safe wording:** "Netflix offered to sell itself to Blockbuster for $50 million. Hastings named the price himself: 'Fifty million.'"
- https://builtin.com/articles/netflix-blockbuster-buyout
- https://www.yahoo.com/news/blockbuster-ceo-struggled-not-laugh-191553438.html
- CNBC via NBC San Diego: https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/netflix-didnt-kill-blockbuster-how-netflix-almost-lost-the-movie-rental-wars/2493680/?amp=1

### 3. "John Antioco was struggling not to laugh." / "The dot-com hysteria is completely overblown." — CAREFUL
Both lines come from Randolph's memoir. The first is Randolph's reading of Antioco's face, not a laugh anyone heard. The second is Antioco's words as Randolph remembers them. Antioco's own account of the meeting was not found.
- **Safe wording:** "According to Netflix co-founder Marc Randolph, Blockbuster's CEO was 'struggling not to laugh,' and told them 'the dot-com hysteria is completely overblown.'"
- https://builtin.com/articles/netflix-blockbuster-buyout
- https://www.yahoo.com/news/blockbuster-ceo-struggled-not-laugh-191553438.html
- Fortune (Apr 14, 2023): https://fortune.com/2023/04/14/netflix-cofounder-marc-randolph-recalls-blockbuster-rejecting-chance-to-buy-it/

### 4. "Laughed us out of their office" — REPORTED
CNBC reports that former Netflix CFO Barry McCarthy, who was in the room, said in a 2008 interview that Blockbuster basically "laughed us out of their office." Search results attribute a fuller version to Variety (2013): "just about laughed us out of their office." That page is paywalled and could not be opened, so do not use the fuller version. Randolph's own 2023 wording on Twitter was different: "laughed us out of the room" (Fortune).
- **Safe wording:** "Netflix's former CFO later said Blockbuster basically 'laughed us out of their office.'" Credit the quote to McCarthy. Do not credit it to Randolph or Hastings.
- https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/netflix-didnt-kill-blockbuster-how-netflix-almost-lost-the-movie-rental-wars/2493680/?amp=1
- https://fortune.com/2023/04/14/netflix-cofounder-marc-randolph-recalls-blockbuster-rejecting-chance-to-buy-it/

### 5. The loafers (drawable) — REPORTED
Randolph on Antioco: "He was dressed casually but expensively." And: "No suit, but his loafers probably cost more than my car." The Independent's summary says the Netflix team was "clad in T-shirts and flip-flops." That wording comes from a newspaper summary, not a direct quote from the book, so treat the Netflix team's clothing as a loose visual cue only. The flight home took them "back to Santa Barbara," and Randolph tapped a champagne flute with a plastic spoon before giving a toast.
- **Safe wording / visual:** Blockbuster's CEO in expensive loafers and no suit. A casually dressed Netflix team. A steel-and-glass Dallas tower.
- https://builtin.com/articles/netflix-blockbuster-buyout
- https://www.yahoo.com/news/blockbuster-ceo-struggled-not-laugh-191553438.html

### 6. In 2000, Netflix lost more than its own asking price — VERIFIED (primary, SEC)
Netflix's 2002 IPO filing (S-1) shows a 2000 net loss of **$57.4 million** ($57,363K) on total revenues of **$35.9 million** ($35,894K). In 1999 it lost $29.8 million. The S-1 table puts subscribers at 292 at the end of 2000. The figure is in thousands, so about 292,000.
- **Safe wording:** "That year, Netflix lost $57 million, more than the $50 million it was asking for."
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065280/000101287002001044/ds1.txt

### 7. Blockbuster at its peak: about 9,100 stores, $6.1 billion — VERIFIED (primary, SEC)
Blockbuster's 2004 10-K says "approximately 9,100 stores in the United States, its territories and 24 other countries as of December 31, 2004." The exact count was 9,094: 7,265 company-operated and 1,829 franchised. 2004 revenue was $6.1 billion ($6,053.2M). The company "employed about 84,300 persons."
- **Safe wording:** "At its peak in 2004, Blockbuster had about 9,000 stores and made $6 billion a year."
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001085734/000119312505063510/d10k.htm
- Fortune and Seattle Met also give "9,000" or "more than 9,000": https://www.seattlemet.com/travel-and-outdoors/2024/05/last-blockbuster-video-bend-oregon

### 8. Late fees: the money Blockbuster gave up — VERIFIED (primary) / REPORTED ($800M)
Blockbuster announced the "end of late fees" at its company-operated U.S. stores from January 1, 2005. Its own 10-K estimated that, in 2005, extended viewing fees "would have directly contributed an estimated $400 million to $450 million in revenues" and about $250–300 million in operating income. It also budgeted about $50 million in marketing costs for the change. CNBC separately reports Blockbuster collected **$800 million in late fees in 2000** (one source).
- **Safe wording:** "Dropping late fees cost Blockbuster an estimated $400 million a year in revenue, by its own estimate." Use the $800M figure only as "CNBC reports $800 million in late fees in 2000."
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001085734/000119312505063510/d10k.htm
- https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/netflix-didnt-kill-blockbuster-how-netflix-almost-lost-the-movie-rental-wars/2493680/?amp=1

### 9. Blockbuster nearly won online — VERIFIED (750K) / REPORTED (rest)
Blockbuster's 10-K reports "more than 750,000 BLOCKBUSTER ONLINE subscribers" as of March 9, 2005. CNBC reports that Blockbuster Online reached 2 million subscribers by the end of 2006. In 2006 it launched Total Access, which let subscribers return discs in stores, at a cost of about $2 per exchange. In one quarter of 2007, Netflix lost 55,000 subscribers. Hastings said in 2009: "If it hadn't been for their debt, they could have killed us." CNBC also reports that Blockbuster carried about $1 billion of debt after its 2004 spin-off from Viacom.
- **Safe wording:** "Blockbuster's online service hit 2 million subscribers. Reed Hastings later admitted: 'If it hadn't been for their debt, they could have killed us.'"
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001085734/000119312505063510/d10k.htm
- https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/netflix-didnt-kill-blockbuster-how-netflix-almost-lost-the-movie-rental-wars/2493680/?amp=1

### 10. Bankruptcy: September 23, 2010 — VERIFIED
Blockbuster and its U.S. subsidiaries filed for Chapter 11 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. At the time it had about 3,000 U.S. stores, all still open, and "nearly $1 billion" in debt. The plan aimed to cut debt to "an estimated $100 million or less." CEO Jim Keyes said: "We are pleased to enter this process with the support of the Senior Noteholders."
- **Safe wording:** "On September 23, 2010, Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy, carrying nearly $1 billion in debt."
- Engadget: https://www.engadget.com/2010-09-23-blockbuster-files-for-chapter-11.html
- UPI: https://www.upi.com/Business_News/2010/09/23/Blockbuster-busted-but-plans-a-rebirth/21491285269470/

### 11. Sold at auction for $320 million — VERIFIED
In April 2011, Dish Network won the bankruptcy auction with a bid of about $320 million (about $228 million in cash after adjustments). It beat Carl Icahn's $310.6 million bid. At that point Blockbuster had "more than 1,700 store locations." The sale price was about 6.4 times the $50 million Netflix had asked for in 2000 (320 ÷ 50 = 6.4).
- **Safe wording:** "In 2011, what was left of Blockbuster sold at auction for about $320 million."
- Fox Business (Apr 6, 2011): https://foxbusiness.com/features/dish-network-wins-bid-for-blockbuster-assets
- Broadcasting & Cable / NextTV (Apr 6, 2011): https://nexttv.com/news/dish-wins-blockbuster-auction-111805

### 12. Netflix today: $45.2 billion a year, 325M+ memberships — VERIFIED (primary, SEC)
Netflix's Q4 2025 shareholder letter (Jan 20, 2026) says: "we delivered $45.2B of revenue" in 2025 (+16%). It says Netflix "crossed the 325M paid memberships milestone during the quarter." That is the latest membership figure, because Netflix no longer reports memberships every quarter. The Q3 2026 letter (Oct 20, 2026) forecasts 2026 revenue of $51.1B. A separate fact from Netflix's Q2 2026 10-Q: Warner Bros. - **Safe wording:** "Last year Netflix brought in $45.2 billion, from more than 325 million paid memberships."

### 13. Netflix killed its own DVD business — VERIFIED (primary)
On April 18, 2023, co-CEO Ted Sarandos announced that Netflix "will be shipping our final discs on September 29, 2023." His post also said: "Those iconic red envelopes changed the way people watched shows and movies at home."
- **Safe wording / visual:** "In 2023, Netflix mailed its last red envelope." The last Blockbuster still rents DVDs (fact 14).
- https://about.netflix.com/en/news/netflix-dvd-the-final-season

### 14. The last Blockbuster on Earth: Bend, Oregon — VERIFIED
The store is at 211 NE Revere Ave, in a strip mall northeast of downtown Bend. Ken and Debbie Tisher opened it in 1992 as a Pacific Video store. It became a Blockbuster franchise in 2000, the same year as the Netflix meeting. It became the last Blockbuster in the U.S. after the two Alaska stores closed in 2018, and the last on Earth after the Perth, Australia store closed in March 2019. All corporate-owned stores had closed by 2014. Netflix's documentary *The Last Blockbuster* premiered on the platform on March 15, 2021, and peaked as the No. 4 movie in the U.S. The store was still open on Oct 18, 2025, when it hosted a 40th-anniversary block party for the brand (Blockbuster was founded in 1985).
- **Safe wording:** "Today, one Blockbuster is left on the planet, a franchise store in Bend, Oregon."
- AP via OPB (Mar 30, 2021): https://www.opb.org/article/2021/03/30/world-s-last-blockbuster-more-popular-after-netflix-show/
- Seattle Met (May 29, 2024): https://www.seattlemet.com/travel-and-outdoors/2024/05/last-blockbuster-video-bend-oregon
- Central Oregon Daily News (Oct 18, 2025): https://www.centraloregondaily.com/news/local/blockbuster-bend-40-anniversary-party/article_0a455b7d-b747-4b26-9713-453428e6acd0.html

### 15. What the last store looks like (drawable) — VERIFIED
The AP described "popcorn ceilings, low fluorescent lighting, wire metal video racks" and the "yellow-and-blue ticket stub logo." The computer system is rebooted from "floppy disks," backups go to a "reel-to-reel tape," and a broken dot-matrix printer means membership cards are written by hand. Seattle Met (2024) says the "movie-ticket-shaped Blockbuster sign still illuminates at night," the merch is "a sea of blue and yellow," and VHS is now limited to "the bargain for-sale bin." Manager Sandi Harding on why it survived: "It's pure stubbornness, for one. We didn't want to give in."
- **Safe wording / visual:** a blue-and-yellow ticket-stub sign glowing over a strip mall, wire racks, fluorescent tubes, a hand-written membership card.

## Myths

1. **MYTH: "Blockbuster tried to buy Netflix and backed out" / "Netflix tried to buy Blockbuster."**
   **True:** Netflix offered to **sell itself** to Blockbuster for $50M. Blockbuster's lawyer asked what number Netflix wanted, and Hastings named it. InsideHook ran a correction after getting this backwards.
   Sources: https://builtin.com/articles/netflix-blockbuster-buyout · https://www.insidehook.com/daily_brief/tv-home-theater/netflix-once-tried-to-buy-blockbuster-for-50-million

2. **MYTH: "Blockbuster literally laughed Netflix out of the room."**
   **True:** No one was thrown out. Randolph says Antioco was "struggling not to laugh," and McCarthy said Blockbuster "basically laughed us out of their office." Both are descriptions, not events. The Netflix team flew home after a polite rejection.
   Sources: https://builtin.com/articles/netflix-blockbuster-buyout · https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/netflix-didnt-kill-blockbuster-how-netflix-almost-lost-the-movie-rental-wars/2493680/?amp=1

3. **MYTH: "Saying no to $50M is what killed Blockbuster" / "Netflix killed Blockbuster."**
   **True:** In 2000 Netflix was losing more than $50M a year (fact 6). Blockbuster later built its own online service with 2M+ subscribers and Total Access, and briefly hurt Netflix. CNBC's account says the decline came from about $1B of debt from the 2004 Viacom spin-off, a boardroom fight with Carl Icahn, and the online push being abandoned after Antioco left in 2007. Hastings said: "If it hadn't been for their debt, they could have killed us."
   Source: https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/netflix-didnt-kill-blockbuster-how-netflix-almost-lost-the-movie-rental-wars/2493680/?amp=1

4. **MYTH: "Netflix was born because Reed Hastings got a $40 late fee on *Apollo 13*."**
   **True:** That is Hastings's story. His co-founder Randolph writes that the overdue *Apollo 13* happened but that the idea "had nothing to do with late fees." He calls the story "emotionally true" but not "the whole story," and notes that Netflix itself charged late fees at first. Use it only as "the story Hastings tells."
   Source: Randolph excerpt (WBUR On Point): https://www.wbur.org/onpoint/2019/11/07/netflix-marc-rudolph-that-will-never-work

5. **MYTH: "The last Blockbuster is a holdout corporate store."**
   **True:** It is a locally owned franchise. Ken and Debbie Tisher opened it as Pacific Video in 1992 and converted it in 2000. Corporate Blockbuster stores were all gone by 2014.
   Source: https://www.opb.org/article/2021/03/30/world-s-last-blockbuster-more-popular-after-netflix-show/

## Strongest hook number
**$50 million is less than 10 hours of Netflix's revenue today.** In 2025, Netflix took in $45.2B (SEC, fact 12). That is about $5.2M an hour ($45.2B ÷ 8,760 hours), so $50M equals about 9.7 hours. Equivalently, Netflix's 2025 revenue is about 900 times the asking price ($45.2B ÷ $50M ≈ 904).

## Strongest twist
**The "no" was not what sank Blockbuster.** In 2000, Netflix was losing $57M a year, more than its own price tag. Six years later, Blockbuster's online service was gaining on Netflix and costing it subscribers. Hastings's own verdict: "If it hadn't been for their debt, they could have killed us." What really sank Blockbuster was debt and a boardroom fight that killed its online push.
Closing irony: Netflix mailed its last red envelope in 2023, while the last Blockbuster in Bend still rents DVDs.

Netflix numbers will move soon. Full-year 2026 results, due around January 2027, will replace the $45.2B figure (current forecast $51.1B), and the 325M membership count will change at Netflix's next milestone announcement.
