Build a YouTube Short in Remotion from my voice-over: Episode 6 of "Billion-Dollar Mistakes", "Blockbuster Could've Bought Netflix for $50 Million".

THE GOAL
It must look like a premium cinematic animated documentary short: painterly, richly textured, deep, lit like a film, with character acting so natural it feels observed from real life. Character design keeps the clean appeal of a high-end anime feature, but every frame is rendered with volume, texture and atmosphere. It must never look like flat vector art, motion graphics, an explainer, a slideshow or a paper cut-out puppet. It plays as one continuous story that moves with the voice: the same world, the same characters and the same hero prop carry the viewer from shot to shot, and every shot hands off to the next. Every cut, every action and every piece of text is synced to the voice-over to the frame.

Work like a real feature-animation studio, in this order, and do not skip ahead:
timing → reference (motion and hands) → design (model sheets, hand sheet, props, texture kit, background plates) → layout and animatic → key poses → breakdowns and in-betweens → secondary motion → lighting, texture and compositing → QA.
Each stage has a quality gate (STEP 12). Do not start a stage until the previous gate passes.

PRIORITIES WHEN SOMETHING HAS TO GIVE
1. Sync and facts are never compromised.
2. Believable movement and hands. A character who moves with real weight in a simple frame beats a detailed character who moves like a paper puppet.
3. Quality over quantity. Fewer, better drawings, well staged, beat many crude ones. Use real animation staging cheats freely: silhouettes against bright windows, backs of heads, over-the-shoulder framing, characters cropped by foreground objects, and moving holds.
4. Every frame must survive a pause. If a paused frame looks like a vector explainer, a PowerPoint slide, clip art or a cut-out puppet, it fails.

STEP 1 — TIMING MAP (do this first)
1. Get the exact audio duration with getAudioDurationInSeconds from @remotion/media-utils and confirm it with ffprobe (they must agree within one frame).
2. Composition "Blockbuster": 1080x1920 at 30 fps. durationInFrames = Math.ceil(duration * 30) + 4. The last 4 frames are the loop hold (see the last shot).
3. Transcribe: make a 16 kHz mono WAV copy of the voice-over in a temp folder (whisper.cpp requires it). Install whisper.cpp and the large-v3-turbo model with @remotion/install-whisper-cpp and transcribe with tokenLevelTimestamps: true; use the DTW token timestamps (t_dtw) where present. Merge sub-word tokens into words.
4. Align Whisper's words to the script below with a sequence alignment. The script wins: "Blockbuster" may come out as "Block buster" (two words) and "Blockbuster's" as "Blockbusters", "Netflix's" as "Netflix" or "Netflixes", "Reed" as "Read", "Hastings" as "Haystings" or "Hastings'", "co-founder" as "cofounder" or "co founder", "ex-CFO" as "ex CFO", "X CFO" or "XCFO", "CFO" as "C.F.O.", "Bend" as "Ben" and "DVDs" as "DVD's" or "D.V.D.s"; numbers may come out as digits ("$50 million", "50 million", "2000", "$57 million", "9,000", "$6 billion", "2 million", "2010", "$45.2 billion", "10 hours"), and one digit token may cover several script words ("2000" for "two thousand", "2010" for "twenty ten", "57" for "fifty-seven", "45.2" for "forty-five point two"); map them to the script's words. Save src/data/blockbuster/words.json as [{ word, scriptIndex, startMs, endMs, rawFrame, frame }].
5. Onset refinement: Whisper word starts are often 40–120 ms off. Decode the voice-over to PCM, compute a 10 ms RMS envelope, and snap every cut word and beat word to the nearest speech onset within ±3 frames. Run ffmpeg silencedetect (noise=-35dB:d=0.18) and record every pause. Keep both the raw and the refined frame.
6. Build src/data/blockbuster/timeline.ts: for every shot its cut word, cut frame, end frame (the next shot's cut frame) and every beat word inside it with its refined frame, plus helpers such as beat("s05", "word"). All animation reads from this file. Never hard-code seconds or absolute frames in components; offsets relative to a beat (beat - 3) are fine.
7. Cut rule: a shot cuts in on its CUT word (usually the first word of the line). Actions land on their BEAT words. A big hit never lands on a shot's first frame: leave at least 6 frames between the cut and the first big hit so the anticipation reads.
8. Sync rule: every action tied to a word lands ON that word's refined start frame. Big actions get 2–4 frames of anticipation before the hit (a wind-up, a squash, a held breath), then follow-through and settle over 6–12 frames after it.
9. Pace: a new shot or a clear new action every 2–3 seconds. If a shot runs past 3 seconds on my voice, add a camera change or a new action on its next beat word. Something meaningful moves at least every second.
10. Write out/blockbuster/timing-report.md (shot, cut word, word time, raw frame, refined frame, every beat word → frame, every pause with its length) and out/blockbuster/sfx-cues.csv (timecode HH:MM:SS:FF at 30 fps, frame, shot, action, suggested sound: elevator chime, felt-tip squeak, ledger slam, heavy report thud, rubber-stamp slam, CRT power-on whine, wall-clock tick, keep-case snap…) so I can add SFX by hand.

RENDERING STYLE (painterly and cinematic, not flat cel)
- Line art is thin, coloured and secondary to light: lines use the darkest local colour (never pure black), are heavier in shadow and on outer contours, thin out or break where light hits an edge, and taper at the ends (build them as filled shapes, not uniform strokes). Forms are defined mainly by light and shadow.
- A reusable <ShadingStack> renders every character part and prop in this order: base colour → form shadow with a soft terminator (2–6 px feathered edge; colour = base tinted by the scene's shadow colour, never grey) → a darker core shadow and ambient occlusion in creases (under the chin and hair, inside the elbow, between the fingers, where a body meets a seat) → on skin, a warm, slightly saturated band along the terminator → bounce light on undersides from the floor or nearby colours → highlight → rim light in the key light's colour → specular glints on eyes, hair, lips, nails, a watch and phone glass.
- Store the key-light direction per shot in style.ts; shadow and light shapes follow it, so a character who turns toward the light brightens on that side.
- Cast shadows: characters and props cast soft shadows onto floors, walls, desks and each other, matching the key light's direction and softness. Contact shadows sit wherever something touches a surface: the Founder's palms flat on the walnut table, the legal pad, the pen and the disc lying on the table, the annual report landing on the boardroom table, and the keep-case leaning on the store's window ledge.

DEPTH (every shot)

PAINTED BACKGROUNDS

- One key light, one fill, and practical lights (neon, screens, lamps) casting coloured pools on nearby surfaces.
- Background plates: render each background layer once as a high-resolution transparent PNG through its own composition (Plate-s01-far, Plate-s01-mid…) with `npx remotion still`, saved to public/plates/blockbuster/. - Skinned body: never rotate rigid SVG pieces around pins. The torso outline follows the three spine bones the same way, so the chest and waist bend and twist. No seams, gaps or overlapping circles at joints, at any angle.
- Two-bone IK with pole vectors for arms and legs: hands are placed on their targets (the disc and its paper sleeve, the elevator call button, the pen and its cap, the legal pad, the walnut table top, the folio, the notebook, the marker and its tray, the ledger's page and cover, the price tag's string, the map pin and the picture-light switch, the annual report, the rubber stamp and its ink pad, the court filing, the cleaning cloth, the keep-case and the window ledge) and feet are planted; a foot carrying weight never slides.
- 2.5D face rig: the eyes, brows, nose, mouth, ears and hairline sit on a virtual ellipsoid and move with head yaw and pitch. During a turn the far eye narrows and moves toward the edge, the nose moves toward the silhouette, the cheek and jaw contour change, the ear shifts, and the hair clumps layer over and under accordingly. For turns beyond about 45°, swap to a dedicated 3/4 or profile head at the fastest point of the turn, where the motion hides the swap.
- Torso turns work the same way: lapels, buttons, belts and seams slide across the torso shape.
- Pose library per character, stored as joint angles + IK targets + face values + hand poses in src/data/blockbuster/poses/.
- Every part draws its own shading stack and texture (STEP 2) from the shot's key-light direction.

HANDS (treat them as their own sub-project)
- Proportions: the hand is about as long as the face from chin to hairline, and the palm about as long as the middle finger. The middle finger is longest; index and ring are about equal; the pinky's tip reaches the last knuckle of the ring finger. The knuckles form an arc, not a straight line. The thumb starts at the wrist, its tip reaches about the middle knuckle of the index finger, and it sits at roughly 90° to the palm (it opposes the fingers; it never lies flat beside them).
- Hand rig: wrist, palm, thumb (3 joints: base, middle, tip) and four fingers with 3 joints each. Each finger is one continuous skinned shape built from its bones with a width profile: slight bulges at the knuckles, a narrower middle, a rounded, padded tip, tapering toward the end.
- Detail: the back of the hand shows nails (correct shape, a highlight, a cuticle line), knuckle creases and wrinkles, and on older characters tendons, veins and softer, older skin; the palm shows the thumb's padded base, the main creases and fingertip pads. Hands get the same shading stack, texture, cast shadows and contact shadows as the rest of the body.
- Views: back, palm, thumb side, pinky side and 3/4. Choose the view per pose so the hand reads clearly; never show a hand edge-on where the fingers merge into one shape.
- Curl rules: when fingers curl, the joints fold progressively (base least, tip most), curled fingers converge toward a point near the base of the palm, and each finger toward the pinky curls slightly more than the one before. Relaxed hands are never flat: the fingers rest in a gentle cascade of curls.
- Grips are layered: the object sits between the hand's layers (palm and far fingers behind it, near fingers and thumb in front), the fingers flatten slightly where they press, and contact shadows sit where skin meets the object. Disc, bare: held by its rim between the thumb pad and the middle finger, the index fingertip resting in the centre hole, never touching the silver face. Disc in its paper sleeve: spun between the index and middle fingertips, then caught flat between the thumb and index. Pen: a tripod grip between thumb, index and middle finger, the side of the hand resting on the pad; the cap pinched in the other hand. Marker: a fist-like pinch between thumb and index, the middle finger supporting it underneath, ring and pinky curled against the palm. Legal pad: a flat palm on the top sheet, four fingers spread, sliding it. Folio: held against the chest, four fingers wrapped around its spine and the thumb on the cover, then tucked under the arm. Notebook: held open on the forearm, the thumb across the page, the pen in the other hand. Ledger: a fingertip running down the column, then a flat palm on the cover for the slam. Price tag: its red string pinched between thumb and index, the tag dangling and swinging. Map pin: its head pinched between the thumb tip and the index tip, the thumb pad pressing it home. Annual report: both hands at its lower corners for the toss, then four fingertips on the cover sliding it. Rubber stamp: the palm cupped over the round wooden knob, the fingers wrapped down the handle, the thumb braced on the far side. Court filing: pushed with a flat palm and spread fingers. Cleaning cloth: bunched in the fingertips, the disc held by its rim in the other hand. Keep-case: cradled open in one palm while the other hand presses the disc onto the hub with the middle finger at the centre, then the thumb snaps the lid shut.
- Motion: the wrist leads and the fingers follow 1–3 frames later; when a hand opens or closes, the fingers move in a cascade from index to pinky, never all at once. Fingers never pop between poses; they interpolate through the rig.
- Pose library (from my reference photos when available, see STEP 3B): relaxed, point, button tap, disc spin, rim catch, disc set-down, pen uncap, pen tripod, palms flat on the table, notebook hold, two fingers to the lips, steepled fingers, open-palm gesture, folio tuck, marker uncap, marker write, marker toss, fingertip line run, double tap, string pinch, flat-palm slam, pin push, switch flick, two-hand toss, fingertip slide, cupped stamp knob, flat-palm push, cloth wipe, hub press, lid snap.
- Close-ups (shots 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 18, 19 and 22, and any insert) get the full detail level; wide shots simplify detail but keep correct proportions and silhouettes.

ACTING (observed from life, never puppet-like)
- Nobody is ever perfectly still: breathing moves the chest, shoulders and clavicles (1–2 px, a 3–4 second cycle, a different phase per character), and standing characters make tiny weight shifts between their feet. A held character is a moving hold.
- Eyes lead: in every look or turn, the eyes move first, the head follows 2–4 frames later, then the torso. While watching or thinking, the eyes make small darts (saccades) every 0.5–1.5 seconds. Blinks take 5–7 frames (a fast close, a slower open), come every 2–4 seconds (seeded) and often accompany a head turn or a change of thought.
- Asymmetry: no mirrored poses. Weight sits on one leg with the hips and shoulders tilted in opposite directions; the two hands do different things; the two sides of the face are never identical.
- Faces act in small amounts: brow tension, lid height, the corners of the mouth, a jaw shift. A flicker of the coming emotion appears a few frames before the main expression (a flash of doubt in the eyes before a smile drops).
- Extreme close-ups on the eyes for dramatic beats.
- Mouths change only for expressions (smile, laugh, open surprise, flat). Characters never lip-sync: the narration is a voice-over; they act and react.
- Eyelines: characters look at what matters (the legal pad and the price written on it, the Founder across the table, the CEO at the head of the table, the nameplate, the elevator readout, the whiteboard, the ledger line, the map and its pins, the monitor, the TV above the counter, the wall clock, and the disc in the Clerk's hands), and screen direction stays consistent across cuts.

FRAME RATE
Characters animate on ones (every frame, 30 per second) for smooth, natural movement; the puppet look is fixed with posing, timing, skinning and overlap, not by dropping frames. Camera, particles, lights, text and effects are also on ones.

STEP 5 — CAMERA, LAYOUT AND ANIMATIC
- Build a reusable <Camera3D>: a real perspective camera in CSS 3D (perspective on the root, transform-style: preserve-3d), with every plate, character and prop placed at its true depth (translateZ, scaled to keep its intended framing size). The camera has a position (x, y, z), rotation, a lens (think 24 mm wide, 35 mm, 50 mm, 85 mm portrait) and a focus distance. Push-ins are dollies (the camera moves, so near layers grow faster than far ones), not zooms. Depth of field: each layer's blur comes from its distance to the focus plane and the shot's aperture; rack focus eases over 8–16 frames with a hint of focus breathing (1–2% scale). Handheld drift from noise2D in @remotion/noise (2–4 px, at most 0.3°), seeded per shot, with a documentary-camera feel: slow and organic on handheld shots, almost none on tripod shots.
- Depth layers: 5–8 in every exterior, 4+ in every interior, and in most shots a foreground element near the lens that crosses the frame (the rain-beaded lamppost and the parked car's hood, the lobby ficus leaves, the elevator door edges, the Co-founder's shoulder, the water carafe, the heavy conference-room door, the CEO's raised hand, the closing ledger cover, drizzle and rain beads on the window glass).
- Every shot has a camera plan: start framing, end framing, move type, easing and the beats that trigger changes. No locked-off camera; a deliberate held beat is still a moving hold (drift + atmosphere).
- Screen direction: progress and time move left to right; the Netflix team crosses the lobby left to right and sits on the left side of the table looking right, while Blockbuster's side looks back right to left; the cuts forward to the table later sweep left to right (the door's shadow, the tower's shadow on "killed") and the whip-pan back to the day of the meeting runs right to left; the annual report flies left to right into shot 14; the camera pans right from the TV to the clock and the window; a whip-pan continues the direction of the motion that motivated it.
- Layout first: write out/blockbuster/layouts.md with one block per shot: framing (wide, medium, close, extreme close), camera move, layers, character key poses with their beat words, every text with its surface, the transition in and out, and the object that hands off to the next shot.
- Animatic: render a fast pass with grey placeholder shapes in the correct positions and the real timing (`--scale=0.25`), watch it against the voice and fix the timing here, before painting and animating.

STEP 6 — MOTION LANGUAGE
- src/lib/ease.ts holds named curves (anticipate, overshoot, settle, slam, whip, float) and a few named spring() configs. Linear motion is allowed only for mechanical constant speed: the elevator readout's arrow ticking, the monitor's counter roll, the CRT's scanline roll, the clock hands' constant sweep until the hard stop, and the drizzle.
- Every move follows anticipation → action → overshoot → settle.
- Breakdowns: between every two key poses, add a breakdown pose that defines the arc and the overlap (never a straight interpolation from key to key). Spacing is slow out of a pose, fast through the middle and slow into the next, and no two body parts start or stop on the same frame.
- Biomechanics: the centre of gravity stays over the supporting foot; before reaching, pulling or lifting, the body shifts its weight first; to lift, the knees bend and the back stays straight; effort shows in the whole body (shoulders tense, neck strains, a short pause before a heavy lift).
- Timing from life at 30 fps (refine from reference): blink 5–7 frames; eye dart 2–3; head turn 8–14; reach for an object 12–20; tap 4–6; both arms up 10–16; hand opening or closing 6–10; sit-to-stand 30–45; the disc spin and catch: 4 frames of wind-up, 8 of spin, a 2–3-frame dead stop between thumb and index; writing "PRICE?" or "$50 MILLION" on the pad: 18–24 frames; the suppressed laugh: the lips press over 6 frames, a 2–3-frame twitch at the mouth corner, the fingers rise to the lips over 12–16; writing "MADE SENSE?" on the whiteboard: 24–36 frames; the ledger slam: 4 frames of lift, 3 of fall, 8–10 of settle with a puff of paper dust; the pin push: 3 frames to press, 4 to release; the report toss: 6 frames of wind-up, 5–6 of flight, a hard landing, 10–12 of settle; the stamp slam: 8 frames of lift and inking, 3 of drop, a 2-frame squash, 8–10 of lift-off; the clock sweep: about 30–40 frames from twelve to the hard stop; the case snap: 3–4 frames.
- Secondary motion is simulated: bake the Founder's open overshirt, the Co-founder's ponytail, the Lawyer's tie, the CFO's visitor badge on its clip, the price tag on its string, the memoir's page corner, the newspaper's fold, the Clerk's hoodie and its drawstrings, and his shaggy hair with a deterministic spring/verlet simulation in scripts/bake-secondary.ts; save per-frame values to src/data/blockbuster/sim/ and read them in the components. Remotion renders frames in parallel, so anything physical must be baked or computed purely from the frame number.
- Weight: heavy things (the annual report, the ledger's cover, the rubber stamp, the heavy conference-room door, the elevator doors) start slowly, stop hard and cause a small shake; light things (the price tag on its string, the memoir's page corner, the newspaper's fold, the disc's paper sleeve, the CFO's visitor badge, dust motes) float, tumble and flutter.
- Arcs: hands, heads and props travel on curved paths (IK targets follow arcs); never slide a hand in a straight line.
- Overlapping action: the head leads, the torso follows 2 frames later, arms 2–4 frames later, hair, hems, bags and loose clothing 2–4 frames after that.
- Smear frames (1–2 frames) on fast moves: the disc spinning in its sleeve (shot 3), the ledger slammed shut (shot 12), the annual report's toss and landing (shots 13, 14), the stamp's drop (shot 18), the clock hands' sweep (shot 20), the keep-case lid snapping shut (shot 22).
- Impact frames (2–3 frames, restrained and filmic: an exposure flash and contrast punch with a touch of speed lines and very slight chromatic aberration) on key hits only, so they keep their punch: "Fifty million" (shot 5, the underline on "million"), "fifty-seven million" (shot 11), "billion" (shot 14, the report landing), "bankruptcy" (shot 18), "forty-five point two billion" (shot 19).
- Screen shake on big hits: decaying over 3–6 frames, maximum 8 px.
- Motion blur with @remotion/motion-blur during fast moves only (whip-pans, the disc's spin (shot 3), the annual report's flight (shots 13, 14), the stamp's drop (shot 18), the clock hands' sweep (shot 20) and the fast pull-back through the window (shot 22)), with a 180° shutter feel and a low sample count to protect render time.
- Particles (dust motes in the sunbeams and dusk beams, paper dust from the ledger slam and the report's landing, drizzle in the parking-lot light, rain beads sliding down the window glass) are deterministic and layered in depth: near ones are bigger, blurrier and faster. Each piece tumbles in 3D (scaleX = cos of its spin) and drifts on its own curve.

STEP 7 — KINETIC TYPOGRAPHY INSIDE THE WORLD
- No captions, no word-by-word subtitles and no floating text cards. Every text sits on a surface in the world.
- <SurfaceText> places text on any four-corner quad using a perspective transform (matrix3d computed from the four corner points), so it sits exactly on its sign, screen, paper or board and moves with that surface's parallax layer.
- Light the text by surface type: emissive (screens, LED, neon: glow and bloom, and it lights nearby surfaces), printed (multiplied by the scene light, paper texture, shadowed), handwritten (marker or chalk texture), stamped.
- Occlusion: foreground objects pass in front of text when natural (the ficus leaves crossing the lobby directory in shot 2, the Lawyer's hand and pen passing over "PRICE?" in shot 4, the water carafe crossing the nameplate in shot 6, the Clerk's arm passing in front of the rack sign in shot 22).
- Entrances land on the exact beat frame and match the surface: LED digits roll and land; neon flickers on (2–3 flickers within 6 frames); screens switch on with a one-frame bright pop; printed text arrives with its object; handwriting is written stroke by stroke; stamps slam with a squash. Exits are motivated: switched off, wiped, washed out, torn, fallen away, scrolled off.
- Handwriting: in a Node script, convert the needed glyphs of Permanent Marker (the .woff from @fontsource/permanent-marker) to outlines with opentype.js and save them to src/data/blockbuster/glyphs.json. Reveal each glyph in writing order through a mask of thick strokes that follow the glyph's centre line, animated with evolvePath from @remotion/paths; the marker or chalk tip follows the leading point (getPointAtLength). Writing finishes on its beat word. If a glyph's centre line is not available, reveal it with a directional wipe along its main stroke, still followed by the pen.
- Fonts via @remotion/google-fonts: "Oswald" for the poster, the lobby directory, the nameplate, the map's label strip, the annual report cover, the monitor, the stamp, the TV, the clock face, the store sign and the rack sign (confirm it carries the ≈ and · glyphs in the stills); "Libre Baskerville" for the memoir page, the newspaper pull quote and the quote card; "Courier Prime" for the visitor badge and the typed ledger line; "VT323" for the elevator's floor readout; "Permanent Marker" for the legal pad, the whiteboard and the price tag. Load only the weights you use, and confirm in the stills that no fallback font appears. Fit long lines with fitText from @remotion/layout-utils.
- Readability on a phone: every key text and key action stays inside x 48–888, y 288–1248 of the 1080×1920 frame (clear of the Shorts buttons and title). Main words have a cap height of at least 72 px; secondary text (small print, attributions, source tags, sticky notes) at least 34 px and on screen for at least 1.2 seconds. Every text has strong contrast with what is behind it. Background art can fill the whole frame.
- Add a showSafeZone prop that draws the safe box, for QA stills only.

STEP 8 — TRANSITIONS
- Lay the shots out on an absolute timeline (<Sequence from={cutFrame}> with premounting), and build each transition as an overlay that straddles the cut, so the audio timeline never shifts. If you use TransitionSeries from @remotion/transitions, remember that transitions overlap the shots and shorten the total: compensate the durations so every cut still lands exactly on its cut frame, and verify it.
- Allowed transitions, each motivated by the story:
  - Whip-pan (6–8 frames): accelerate out with motion blur and speed lines, cut at maximum blur on the cut frame, decelerate in, continuing the screen direction.
  - Light-flash cut: a bright source in shot A blooms toward white over 3–4 frames; cut; shot B blooms down from its own brightest light over 4–6 frames.
  - Match cut: a shape or motion in shot A matches the position, scale and direction of one in shot B on the exact cut frame.
  - Continuous camera move: the camera travels from one setup to the next without a cut.
  - Foreground wipe: an object passes the lens, fills the frame and the cut happens behind it.
- Never crossfades, dips to black or generic slides.

STEP 10 — COMPOSITING AND FINISH
- Layer order in every shot: plates → haze cards between depth layers → practical light pools → cast and contact shadows → characters (shading stack, texture, rim light, light wrap) → in-world text → atmosphere (particles) → light beams → glow, bloom and halation on emissive surfaces → lens flares → grade → grain → vignette → chromatic aberration (impact frames only).
- Bloom: a blurred copy of emissive layers composited with a screen blend.
- Morning sun shafts through the lobby glass wall and across the conference table: long, soft-edged gold shafts with dust motes drifting in them. Low dusk beams raking across the empty table later: warm orange-pink, crisp near the window and softening across the walnut. The brass picture light's warm cone over the map and the cold downlight cone over the table in 2010, both with dust hanging in them. Outside the store, the lamppost's sodium cone and the window's fluorescent spill: wet, visible cones with drizzle streaking through them.
- Screen glow on faces: a coloured soft-light shape on the side of the face toward the screen that follows the screen's brightness.
- Lens flares on the morning sun through the glass walls, the low dusk sun, the lamppost, the store sign as it flickers on and the TV's power-on pop.
- Film finish in a reusable <Grade> (per-scene settings in style.ts): a filmic contrast curve with slightly lifted blacks and a soft highlight roll-off; split toning (shadows toward the scene's ambient colour, highlights toward the key light); halation (a thin warm-red glow around the brightest highlights and practical lights); light wrap (bright backgrounds bleed a few pixels over character edges); very subtle gate weave (at most 0.5 px).
- Grain: reuse the earlier grain component, changing every frame, stronger in mid-tones and shadows than in highlights, at 4–7% opacity; pre-render a set of grain tiles and cycle them if per-frame turbulence slows the render. A gentle lens vignette (10–15%) with slight edge softness.
- Keep renders fast: no full-frame SVG filters every frame; prefer pre-rendered plates, transforms and opacity.

AVOID (this is what makes it look cheap)
- Flat single-colour backgrounds, uniform line weight, pure black lines, grey shadows.
- Clean vector surfaces: flat fills, plastic gradients, razor-sharp shadow edges with no texture, everything in focus at the same depth.
- Elements that pop, scale or fade in from the centre like a slideshow; text floating in space.
- Characters built from circles and rectangles; paper cut-out motion (rigid pieces rotating on pins, visible joint seams, the whole body moving as one block).
- Mitten or sausage hands, fingers of equal length, a flat thumb lying beside the fingers, fingers that open or close all at once, objects floating in front of a hand instead of being gripped.
- Floaty, evenly spaced in-betweens with no breakdown poses; sliding feet; mirrored (twinned) poses; perfect stillness.
- Linear easing on organic motion; everything starting on the same frame; everything moving at once.
- Locked-off cameras and frozen frames.
- Any fact, number, date or name not in this prompt.
