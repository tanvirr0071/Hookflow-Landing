# McDonald's is a landlord: facts

Series: Markup. Research date: 2026-10-23. Newest full year: **fiscal 2025** (year ended Dec 31, 2025). Results released Feb 11, 2026; the 10-K was filed Feb 2026. No full-year 2026 figures exist yet.

Lead facts from the brief: 95% franchised, $16.5B of $26.9B, 56% of land and 80% of buildings all **check out** against the FY2025 10-K. The "real estate business" quote does **not** check out the way it is usually told (see fact 13 and the myths).

## Facts

1. **VERIFIED: about 95% of McDonald's restaurants are franchised.** The 10-K says: "Of the 45,356 McDonald's restaurants at year-end 2025, approximately 95% were franchised." It also says 95% in the U.S.
   - Counts: 43,317 franchised and **2,039 company-operated**.
   - U.S. restaurants at year-end 2025: 13,706.
   - Safe wording: "Of about 45,000 McDonald's worldwide, McDonald's itself runs only about 2,000."
   - Sources: 10-K (main document) · R36 (restaurants by ownership type)

2. **VERIFIED: 2025 franchised revenues were $16.548B out of $26.885B total revenue.** Company-operated sales were $9.690B. For 2024 the figures were $15.715B franchised and $25.920B total.
   - Franchised revenues were about 62% of total revenue (my arithmetic).
   - Safe wording: "About $16.5 billion of McDonald's $26.9 billion in revenue last year came from franchisees, not from selling food."
   - Sources: 10-K · Feb 11, 2026 release (same figures)

3. **VERIFIED (hook): rent alone was $10.442B in 2025, far more than royalties of $6.018B.** Initial fees were $88M.
   - Rent was $10.017B in 2024 and $9.840B in 2023.
   - Rent is about **63% of franchised revenue** and about **39% of all McDonald's revenue** (my arithmetic: 10,442/16,548 and 10,442/26,885).
   - Safe wording: "Last year McDonald's collected about $10.4 billion in rent from its own franchisees. That's almost twice what it got in royalties."
   - Source: 10-K note "Franchise Arrangements", revenue table: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/R15.htm

4. **VERIFIED: McDonald's owns about 56% of the land and about 80% of the buildings under restaurants in its consolidated markets.** The figure is the same at Dec 31, 2025 and 2024.
   - Safe wording: "In the markets it runs directly, McDonald's owns the dirt under more than half its restaurants and about 80% of the buildings."
   - Do not drop "in its consolidated markets": the figure does not cover all 45,000 restaurants.
   - Source: 10-K (Properties section)

5. **VERIFIED: how the deal works.** A conventional franchise "generally include[s] a lease and a license." The franchisee pays initial fees plus continuing rent and royalties, based on a percent of sales, with minimum rent payments.
   - McDonald's "generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the land and building."
   - Franchisees generally also pay occupancy costs, including property taxes, insurance and site maintenance.
   - Safe wording: "The franchisee buys the right to run the restaurant. McDonald's is usually their landlord, and the rent goes up when sales go up."
   - Source: 10-K (Properties section) and Franchise Arrangements note (R15, above)

6. **VERIFIED (twist): a franchise generally lasts 20 years, and McDonald's keeps the real estate.** The note says franchisees get the right to operate "generally for a period of 20 years."
   - At the end of the term, McDonald's keeps control of the land and building. It can sign a new 20-year deal with the same franchisee or a new one, or close the restaurant.
   - Safe wording: "After 20 years the franchise ends, but the building still belongs to McDonald's."
   - Source: 10-K Franchise Arrangements note: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/R15.htm

7. **VERIFIED: franchisees already owe McDonald's $31.451B in future minimum rent** under existing conventional franchise deals.
   - Of that, $15.843B is on sites McDonald's owns and $15.608B on sites it leases.
   - $3.045B is due in 2026 alone.
   - These are minimums only; percentage rent comes on top.
   - Safe wording: "McDonald's has more than $31 billion in rent already locked in from its franchisees."
   - Source: 10-K Franchise Arrangements note, future gross minimum rent payments table (R15, above)

8. **VERIFIED: the landlord side earns about 10 times the profit of running restaurants.**
   - 2025 franchised margins: **$13.930B**. Company-operated margins: **$1.422B**, on $9.69B of sales.
   - The 10-K: "Franchised margins represented approximately 90% of restaurant margin dollars."
   - Safe wording: "The 2,000 restaurants McDonald's runs made it about $1.4 billion. The 43,000 it rents out made it about $13.9 billion."
   - Source: 10-K MD&A, restaurant margins

9. **VERIFIED: McDonald's books only about a fifth of what customers spend.** Global systemwide sales were **$139.4B** in 2025, up 7% (5% in constant currencies). McDonald's own revenue was $26.9B, about 19% of that (my arithmetic).
   - Safe wording: "Customers spent about $139 billion at McDonald's last year. McDonald's the company booked about $27 billion of it."
   - Sources: 10-K · Feb 11, 2026 release ("over $139 billion")

10. **CAREFUL: the property on the books, at historical cost.**
    - At Dec 31, 2025, property and equipment at cost was $49.29B. That includes **land of $8.169B**, buildings and improvements on owned land of $22.202B, and buildings on leased land of $15.506B.
    - Net property under franchise arrangements was $22.8B, including $7.1B of land.
    - These are purchase-price (cost) figures, not market value. Do NOT say "McDonald's real estate is worth $X billion."
    - Safe wording: "On its books, McDonald's carries about $8 billion of land at what it paid for it."
    - Sources: R14 (property and equipment) · R15 (franchise arrangements)

11. **VERIFIED (visual): the first restaurant Ray Kroc opened.**
    - It opened in **Des Plaines, Illinois, on April 15, 1955**.
    - UPI describes "the tiny building" with "red and white tiles inside and the arches outside."
    - Hamburgers cost **15 cents** and french fries **10 cents**. First-day sales were **$366.12**.
    - Kroc at first hired only boys, and banned jukeboxes and cigarette machines.
    - By 1984 the store still had no indoor seating or bathrooms. A store three times the size was built across the street, and the original came down.
    - Safe wording: "Kroc's first store was a tiny red-and-white tiled stand with no tables inside. Burgers were 15 cents."
    - Sources: UPI, Mar 5, 1984: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1984/03/05/First-Golden-Arches-demolished/9076447310800/ · AP via FOX 32, Nov 21, 2017 (date and site, replica museum razed): https://www.fox32chicago.com/news/mcdonalds-to-demolish-museum-of-1st-restaurant

12. **REPORTED (visual): the signs before the "M".**
    - From 1953 to 1962, McDonald's signs showed **Speedee**, "the little chef with a round hamburger face striding across the sign."
    - The sign was neon with "a single yellow arch."
    - In 1962 it switched to "a pair of golden arches that could also be read as an 'M.'"
    - Around 1965, crew paper hats at the Topeka store also carried Speedee.
    - Safe wording: "Before the golden M, the sign was a single neon arch and a running little chef with a hamburger for a head."
    - Source: Kansas Historical Society (state agency), Kansapedia: https://www.kansashistory.gov/kansapedia/mcdonald-s-sign/10232

13. **CAREFUL: the real estate idea came from Harry Sonneborn, not Ray Kroc.**
    - Sonneborn was Kroc's finance man, a former Tastee-Freez executive, and later McDonald's first president and CEO. He resigned on June 8, 1967.
    - Restaurant Finance Monitor (John Hamburger, 2017): Sonneborn devised the model in which McDonald's controlled the site and leased it to the franchisee at a 40% markup, through Franchise Realty Corporation. It says Sonneborn "would tell skeptical bankers the company was really in the real estate business."
    - Sources give 1955, 1956 and 1957 for when Franchise Realty was formed. Say "in the mid-1950s."
    - Safe wording: "The landlord model was Harry Sonneborn's idea. He told bankers McDonald's was really in the real estate business."
    - Do NOT put a word-for-word quote in his mouth (see myth 1).
    - Sources: https://www.restfinance.com/restaurant-finance-across-america/ray-kroc-not-the-founder-but-a-financial-engineer/article_a3d94b01-60db-5a81-9ac9-6e1ec10d8d28.html · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_J._Sonneborn (background only)

14. **REPORTED: what a U.S. franchisee pays now**, from the 2026 Franchise Disclosure Document as summarized by Franchise Chatter (Oct 11, 2026). I could not open McDonald's own franchising pages (blocked).
    - Initial franchise fee: **$45,000**.
    - Service fee: 4% or 5% of gross sales. Advertising: not less than 4%.
    - Total initial investment: $1.472M to $2.807M.
    - Effective rent for 2025 franchised restaurants ranged from **0% to 33.37% of sales**.
    - Average sales of a U.S. traditional franchised restaurant: $4.057M.
    - Safe wording: "A new U.S. franchisee pays $45,000 just for the license, then rent and fees every month as a slice of sales."
    - Source: https://www.franchisechatter.com/2026/10/11/mcdonalds-franchise-review-2026-costs-fees-news-average-revenues-and-or-profits/

## Common myths

1. **MYTH: "Ray Kroc said, 'I'm not in the hamburger business, I'm in the real estate business.'"**
   - I found no primary record of Kroc saying it.
   - The famous film line ("You're not in the burger business, you're in the real estate business") is spoken by the movie version of Sonneborn in *The Founder* (2016). It is a screenplay, not a record.
   - The longer version is "We are not technically in the food business. We are in the real estate business. The only reason we sell fifteen-cent hamburgers is because they are the greatest producer of revenue from which our tenants can pay us our rent."
     - It is attributed to Sonneborn, and some sites cite John F. Love's *McDonald's: Behind the Arches* (Bantam, 1986).
     - I could not open the book to confirm the wording, the setting or the date. The secondary versions don't agree word for word.
   - Safe use: paraphrase it (fact 13), or leave it out.

2. **MYTH: "McDonald's is a real estate company, not a burger company."**
   - CAREFUL truth: rent is McDonald's single biggest revenue line ($10.4B, about 39% of revenue). But much of that rent is a **percentage of the restaurant's sales**, and McDonald's still sold $9.7B of food in its own restaurants.
   - The burgers are what make the rent payable. Say "McDonald's makes more from rent than from royalties," not "McDonald's doesn't make money from food."

3. **MYTH: "McDonald's owns the land under every restaurant."**
   - It owns about **56% of the land and 80% of the buildings** under restaurants in its consolidated markets, and leases the rest long term.
   - The 20,700+ developmental-licensed and affiliated restaurants (9,675 + 11,072) are outside that figure. Per the 10-K, developmental licensees and affiliates pay McDonald's royalties based on a percent of sales (and generally initial fees), not the lease-plus-license deal.

4. **MYTH: "Ray Kroc founded McDonald's."**
   - Richard and Maurice McDonald ran the original McDonald's in San Bernardino, California. They rebuilt it as a self-service stand in 1948.
   - Kroc franchised their system. His first store opened in Des Plaines in 1955, and he bought the brothers' rights in 1961.
   - Source: McDonald's Corporation release on Richard McDonald's death, Oak Brook, July 14, 1998: https://www.hotel-online.com/news/archive-49126 · UPI 1984 (above)

5. **MYTH: "Franchise royalties are McDonald's main income."**
   - Royalties were $6.0B in 2025. Rent was $10.4B. The landlord fee is bigger than the brand fee.

## Strongest hook number
**$10.4 billion.** That is how much rent McDonald's collected from its own franchisees in 2025, almost twice its royalties and more than its total company-operated restaurant sales ($9.7B) (10-K, R15).

## Strongest twist
The roughly 2,000 restaurants McDonald's runs itself made it about **$1.4B** in margin in 2025. The 43,000 it doesn't run, where it is mostly the landlord, made it **$13.9B**, about 90% of its restaurant margin. And when a 20-year franchise ends, the franchisee walks away and McDonald's still has the building.

## Could change soon
McDonald's Q3 2026 results are due in late October or early November 2026 (the company hadn't confirmed a date in what I found). The share price and market value move daily. Rent percentages and fees change with each yearly Franchise Disclosure Document. Use only the FY2025 annual figures above until the FY2026 10-K comes out (about Feb 2027).
